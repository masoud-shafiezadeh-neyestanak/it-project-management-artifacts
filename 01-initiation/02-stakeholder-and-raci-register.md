## 1. Purpose and Objectives

### 1.1 Purpose

This document identifies the internal and external stakeholders of Project Genesis, evaluates their influence and interest, documents their expectations, and establishes appropriate engagement strategies.

It also defines the Responsibility Assignment Matrix (RACI) to clarify execution responsibilities, accountability, consultation requirements, and information-sharing obligations across the project's work packages.

Project Genesis is a real business and operational readiness initiative undertaken within Bridge of Tomorrow, an Oman-registered company with an active Commercial Registration (CR).

The Stakeholder Register and RACI Matrix provide a consistent basis for stakeholder coordination, decision-making, communication management, and project governance.

### 1.2 Objectives

The objectives of this document are to:

- Identify individuals, functional roles, organizations, and external parties that may influence or be affected by Project Genesis.
- Assess stakeholder power, interest, expectations, and engagement requirements.
- Distinguish confirmed project assignments from roles that remain subject to appointment, engagement, or confirmation.
- Define appropriate communication and stakeholder engagement strategies.
- Establish clear responsibility and accountability for project activities.
- Clarify decision-making and approval authority.
- Maintain alignment between the Project Charter, Scope Baseline, Risk Register, Communication Management Plan, and WBS.
- Reduce ambiguity, duplicated responsibilities, and approval delays.
- Maintain traceable records of stakeholder changes and responsibility assignments.

### 1.3 Stakeholder Management Principles

The following principles govern stakeholder management throughout Project Genesis:

1. Every material project stakeholder must be identified and assigned an appropriate engagement approach.
2. Stakeholder expectations must be understood and managed throughout the project lifecycle.
3. Every project work package must have one designated Accountable owner.
4. Responsibility assignments must remain consistent with the current WBS and applicable project baselines.
5. Functional roles must not be represented as confirmed personnel or active contractors unless their assignment has been verified.
6. External advisers provide specialist advice within their engagements but do not automatically hold internal project approval authority.
7. Regulatory authorities and prospective clients are external stakeholders, not members of the internal project delivery team.
8. Business signing authority and financial delegation remain subject to formal company authorization.
9. Verbal support, discussion, and informal agreement do not replace required written approvals.
10. Stakeholder information and engagement strategies must be reviewed when project conditions or organizational arrangements change.

### 2.3 Power-Interest Analysis

Power and interest are assessed using the following definitions.

**Power:** The stakeholder's ability to influence project decisions, authorize resources, approve changes, affect regulatory compliance, or constrain project delivery.

**Interest:** The stakeholder's level of involvement, concern, or direct interest in the project's objectives and outcomes.

| Power | Interest | Engagement Strategy | Management Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | High | Manage Closely | Maintain active involvement, regular communication, and direct participation in material decisions. |
| High | Low | Keep Satisfied | Provide relevant updates, meet applicable statutory requirements, and avoid unnecessary communication. |
| Low | High | Keep Informed | Maintain regular information sharing and seek feedback when appropriate. |
| Low | Low | Monitor | Monitor changes in interest or influence and engage when necessary. |

Where a stakeholder has Medium power or variable interest, the Project Manager will tailor the engagement strategy according to actual responsibilities, influence, and involvement.

The Power-Interest Grid is a planning and prioritization tool. It does not replace statutory communication obligations, contractual responsibilities, or formal approval requirements.

| Code | Responsibility | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| R | Responsible | Performs the work or produces the required output. |
| A | Accountable | Has ultimate accountability for completion of the activity. Exactly one A must be assigned to each activity. |
| C | Consulted | Provides specialist advice, input, or review. Communication is two-way. |
| I | Informed | Receives updates regarding progress, decisions, or outcomes. Communication is generally one-way. |

RACI accountability for completing a work package does not automatically confer legal signing authority, financial delegation, or authority to approve company contracts.
