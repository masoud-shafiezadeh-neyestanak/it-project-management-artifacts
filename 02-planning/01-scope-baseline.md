## 1. Project Scope Statement

### 1.1 Project Background and Objective

Project Genesis is a real business and operational readiness initiative being implemented within **Bridge of Tomorrow**, an Oman-registered company with an active Commercial Registration (CR).

The project aims to establish and strengthen the company's digital infrastructure, corporate web presence, commercial documentation, operational management workflows, digital service portfolio, and initial client outreach capabilities.

The objective is to create a structured, secure, and operationally manageable business foundation that supports the company's actual activities and future service delivery.

Project Genesis follows a Hybrid Project Management approach, combining structured governance and documented approval controls with iterative implementation, testing, and refinement where appropriate.

The planned delivery period is eight calendar weeks, subject to the formally approved start date, resource availability, budget authorization, stakeholder decisions, and external dependencies.

### 1.2 Scope Baseline Components

The Project Scope Baseline consists of three integrated components:

1. **Project Scope Statement:** Defines the project objectives, deliverables, scope boundaries, assumptions, and constraints.
2. **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS):** Decomposes the total project scope into manageable deliverables and work packages.
3. **WBS Dictionary:** Defines the work packages, responsibilities, acceptance criteria, and planned delivery windows.

The Schedule Baseline and Cost Baseline are maintained as separate project management baselines. They must remain consistent with the approved scope and be managed through coordinated project change control.

This document integrates the Scope Statement, WBS, and WBS Dictionary into a single controlled Scope Baseline.

### 1.3 Project Deliverables

The project comprises seven principal deliverables.

#### D1 — Digital Infrastructure

Establish the company's foundational digital infrastructure, including:

- Corporate domain registration and DNS configuration.
- Corporate email and collaboration workspace.
- Cloud document repository.
- Selection and configuration of appropriate hosting and cloud services.
- Identity and access management controls.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) for supported administrative and business accounts.
- Least-privilege access arrangements.
- Appropriate backup and recovery arrangements.
- Technical documentation and administrative ownership records.
- Baseline security and functional verification.

Google Workspace may be used for corporate email and collaboration, while AWS or another approved provider may be used for hosting and related cloud requirements, subject to the approved architecture, security requirements, and budget.

A secondary domain or separate backup endpoint will only be included if justified by operational requirements, availability, eligibility, cost, and formal approval.

#### D2 — Corporate Web Presence

Design, implement, test, and publish a responsive bilingual corporate website in Arabic and English.

The website will include:

- Approved corporate information and service descriptions.
- A professional and responsive user interface.
- HTTPS configuration.
- Functional business inquiry and contact mechanisms.
- Basic browser compatibility and functional testing.
- Baseline security verification.
- Business-owner review and approval of published content.

The website must accurately represent Bridge of Tomorrow's verified service portfolio.

Search-engine rankings, website traffic, customer conversion, and revenue are not guaranteed project outcomes.

#### D3 — Commercial and Legal Template Suite

Prepare five reusable commercial and legal document templates:

1. Commercial Proposal Template.
2. Statement of Work (SOW).
3. Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA).
4. Master Services Agreement (MSA).
5. Service Level Agreement (SLA).

The templates must be reviewed by relevant business stakeholders and, where appropriate, a qualified legal adviser familiar with applicable Omani requirements.

Required review comments, unresolved legal issues, and proposed corrections must be documented and addressed or formally escalated before the templates are authorized for organizational use.

Approval of standard templates does not replace transaction-specific legal and commercial review for future client engagements.

#### D4 — Operational Workspace

Establish a centralized Asana workspace to support project coordination and operational workflows.

The workspace will include:

- An agreed project and task hierarchy.
- Assigned responsibilities and workflow ownership.
- Task templates and appropriate custom fields.
- Milestone and progress tracking.
- Iterative delivery boards and backlogs where appropriate.
- Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) checklists.
- Appropriate access controls.
- Integration with the organization's documentation and version-control practices.
- Coordination between Asana and GitHub where suitable.

Scrum-specific ceremonies, roles, and practices will only be used where explicitly adopted for the relevant work.

#### D5 — Digital Service Catalogue

Prepare and publish a digital service catalogue describing Bridge of Tomorrow's verified and approved service portfolio.

The catalogue will:

- Describe relevant service lines and intended SME client needs.
- Use activity descriptions verified against the company's current Commercial Registration.
- Include appropriate business contact channels.
- Identify service scope and exclusions where relevant.
- Include pricing information and service-level commitments only after the necessary business and legal approvals.
- Avoid claims that imply authorization for unverified or unlicensed activities.

The catalogue may be published on the corporate website or another approved digital channel.

It must not be published as a definitive representation of authorized company activities until the relevant registration and service information has been verified.

#### D6 — Client Outreach Package

Prepare and dispatch five formal commercial proposals to qualified SME prospects in the agreed Muscat and South Al Batinah target areas.

The work includes:

- Identifying and qualifying suitable target businesses.
- Preparing customized proposals using the approved commercial template.
- Aligning proposals with verified service capabilities and approved commercial terms.
- Recording proposal references, dispatch dates, and follow-up status.
- Retaining appropriate dispatch evidence in a controlled project repository.
- Maintaining appropriate prospect information and communication records.

The objective is to complete and document five proposal dispatches.

The project does not guarantee client responses, signed agreements, revenue, or commercial conversion.

#### D7 — Project Closeout Package

Complete the formal project closeout process, including:

- Deliverable acceptance review.
- Verification of the agreed completion criteria for D1–D6.
- Technical and operational asset handover.
- Documentation of outstanding issues, exceptions, and follow-up actions.
- Final budget summary.
- Lessons Learned Retrospective.
- Preparation of the Project Closeout Report.
- Formal closure approval by the Project Sponsor or authorized delegate.

Any uncompleted deliverable or accepted exception must be documented, assigned an accountable owner, and accompanied by an agreed follow-up action.

### 1.4 Out-of-Scope Activities

The following activities are excluded unless formally introduced through approved change control:

- Construction of residential or non-residential buildings.
- Acquisition of physical real estate or facilities.
- Permanent full-time employee recruitment during the planned eight-week project execution window.
- Custom software products or large-scale enterprise applications outside the approved corporate website scope.
- Live production payment gateway implementation.
- Execution of regulated or otherwise unauthorized commercial activities.
- Import/export transactions beyond the approved operational documentation and procedure-development scope.
- Guaranteed client conversion, executed contracts, revenue, or market share.
- Long-term managed IT services and continuous maintenance beyond the agreed handover arrangements.
- Additional deliverables that have not been approved through the formal change-control process.

Scope-related risks and proposed changes must be managed in accordance with the Risk Register (GEN-RSK-001), including RSK-003 — Scope Creep.

No specific CR activity codes or confidential registration details are reproduced in this public Scope Baseline.

### 1.5 Project Assumptions

The scope and delivery plan are based on the following assumptions:

1. Bridge of Tomorrow maintains an active Commercial Registration, and current registration details and relevant activity descriptions will be made available for verification.
2. Required corporate content and brand assets will be provided in a timely manner.
3. Stakeholders will provide decisions within agreed review periods.
4. A target turnaround of 48 hours for critical reviews will only apply where explicitly agreed with the relevant stakeholders.
5. Required cloud, domain, collaboration, and repository services will be available on acceptable terms, subject to provider conditions and technical suitability.
6. The Project Sponsor will make material scope, budget, and schedule decisions promptly after formal designation.
7. Qualified external legal counsel will be available for the agreed review activities.
8. Required contractors and contributors can be engaged within the authorized cost arrangements.
9. The selected architecture will remain proportionate to the project's operational requirements and financial constraints.
10. The initial budget estimate will be reviewed and refined before it is treated as an authorized cost baseline.
11. The planned eight-calendar-week duration will be validated against the actual working calendar and resource availability.

If an assumption proves incorrect and materially affects delivery, the Project Manager must assess the impact and document any required corrective action or change request.

### 1.6 Project Constraints

#### Financial Constraint

The current preliminary planning estimate is USD 1,500–2,500, approximately OMR 570–960.

The estimate is indicative and is not an approved spending limit or financial commitment.

The authorized spending limit, included cost categories, and delegated spending authority must be documented in the approved Budget Baseline.

Contractor fees, legal expenses, recurring subscriptions, infrastructure costs, administrative charges, applicable taxes, and contingency must be explicitly considered.

No expenditure beyond the company's existing delegated authority may be committed without the required approval.

#### Schedule Constraint

The planned duration is eight calendar weeks, nominally equivalent to 40 business days under a five-day working-week assumption.

The actual baseline start and target completion dates must be confirmed.

Public holidays, contributor availability, supplier lead times, and external approval dependencies must be reflected in the detailed schedule where applicable.

#### Regulatory and Security Constraint

Project activities must comply with applicable Omani legal and regulatory requirements.

Relevant requirements may include commercial registration, activity-specific licensing, personal data protection, taxation, contractual obligations, and information security.

The applicability and implementation of these requirements must be verified with the relevant authorities or qualified legal advisers as appropriate.

Passwords, API keys, private keys, access tokens, and other secrets must not be committed to public GitHub repositories.

#### Resource Constraint

The project will use confirmed internal responsibilities and appropriately engaged contractor arrangements.

Permanent full-time recruitment during the planned execution window is excluded.

Material resource gaps that threaten delivery must be escalated through project governance.

### 2.1 WBS Decomposition Rules

- Each lowest-level WBS item must represent identifiable work with a defined output or completion condition.
- Work must be sufficiently decomposed to support assignment, scheduling, monitoring, and acceptance.
- The WBS describes the project scope; detailed task sequencing and day-to-day execution will be managed in the Schedule Baseline and Asana workspace.
- Work packages must remain traceable to the relevant project deliverable.
- Every work package must have one designated Accountable owner.
- Responsibility assignments must remain consistent with the Stakeholder Register and RACI Matrix (GEN-STK-001).
- Any proposed addition, removal, or material change to the WBS must follow the project's change-control procedure.

**WBS sequencing note:** WBS numbering represents hierarchical decomposition rather than chronological execution order. For the website work, WBS 1.3.4 — Website Quality & Security Testing must be completed before WBS 1.3.3 — Production Website Release is authorized.

### 3.1 Responsibility Definitions

- **Responsible:** The role or individual performing the work and producing the required output.
- **Accountable:** The single role or individual answerable for completion and acceptance of the work package.
- **Consulted:** Stakeholders whose specialist advice or review is required.
- **Informed:** Stakeholders who receive relevant status or decision updates.

Consulted and Informed stakeholders are identified in the Stakeholder Register and RACI Matrix (GEN-STK-001).

The Accountable owner must be confirmed before the relevant work package is authorized.

The Project Sponsor and authorized company representative must be formally designated before their approval responsibilities are treated as operative.

### 3.2 Acceptance and Quality Rules

- Acceptance criteria must be verifiable through documented evidence.
- Completed work must be reviewed by the appropriate designated authority.
- Rejected deliverables must have documented reasons and corrective actions.
- Material defects or unresolved exceptions must be recorded in the project issue record or approved issue log.
- Legal review must not be represented as a blanket certification of regulatory compliance.
- Formal acceptance of a deliverable must be recorded in the approved project workspace or controlled document repository.
- Acceptance of a technical or operational deliverable does not authorize activities outside the company's registered or otherwise applicable legal permissions.

## 5. Governance and Scope Control

### 5.1 Baseline Change Control

The approved Scope Statement, WBS, and WBS Dictionary constitute the Scope Baseline.

The Schedule Baseline and Cost Baseline are maintained as separate but integrated project baselines.

Any proposed change affecting scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria, schedule, budget, quality, security, or regulatory obligations must be documented in a Change Request.

The Project Manager will:

1. Record the proposed change and its rationale.
2. Assess the impact on scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, security, and compliance.
3. Identify dependencies and alternative approaches where appropriate.
4. Submit material changes to the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative for approval.
5. Record the decision and update the applicable baseline documents when approved.
6. Communicate approved changes to affected stakeholders.

No material change is considered approved until the authorized decision has been recorded.

### 5.2 Work Authorization

Before a work package begins, the following must be clear:

- Responsible role and Accountable owner.
- Required inputs and dependencies.
- Planned delivery window.
- Applicable acceptance criteria.
- Required approvals and budget authority.
- Relevant security, privacy, and regulatory considerations.
- Availability of required resources.

Work packages that depend on legal, regulatory, or other formal authorization must not proceed beyond the permitted boundary while that authorization remains outstanding.

### 5.3 Traceability

Each work package must remain traceable across project documentation and execution systems.

- Asana tasks and GitHub issues should reference the corresponding WBS code, for example, `[WBS 1.3.2]`.
- Deliverable documentation must identify the relevant deliverable ID (D1–D7).
- Acceptance evidence and material decisions must be retained in an appropriately controlled repository.
- The Risk Register (GEN-RSK-001) must remain linked to the Scope Baseline and Schedule Baseline where relevant.
- The Stakeholder Register (GEN-STK-001) must reflect the Responsible and Accountable role assignments in the WBS Dictionary.
- Approved scope changes must be reflected in the relevant project records.

### 5.4 Scope Verification

The Project Manager will coordinate scope verification throughout delivery and conduct a formal acceptance review during project closeout.

Scope verification confirms that the completed work meets the agreed scope and acceptance criteria.

It does not, by itself, constitute legal certification, guarantee commercial results, or confirm that excluded activities have been authorized.
