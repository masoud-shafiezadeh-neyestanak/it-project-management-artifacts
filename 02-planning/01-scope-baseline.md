## 1. Project Scope Statement

### 1.1 Project Background and Objective

Project Genesis is a real business and operational readiness initiative being implemented within Bridge of Tomorrow, an Oman-registered company with an active Commercial Registration (CR).

The project aims to establish and strengthen the company's digital infrastructure, corporate web presence, commercial documentation, operational management workflows, digital service portfolio, and initial client outreach capabilities.

The objective is to create a structured, secure, and operationally manageable business foundation that supports the company's actual activities and future service delivery.

Project Genesis follows a Hybrid Project Management approach, combining structured governance and documented approval controls with iterative implementation, testing, and refinement where appropriate.

The planned delivery period is eight calendar weeks, subject to the formally approved start date, resource availability, budget authorization, stakeholder decisions, and external dependencies.

### 1.2 Scope Baseline Components

The Project Scope Baseline consists of three integrated components:

1. **Project Scope Statement:** Defines project objectives, deliverables, scope boundaries, assumptions, and constraints.
2. **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS):** Decomposes the total project scope into manageable deliverables and work packages.
3. **WBS Dictionary:** Defines work packages, responsibilities, acceptance criteria, and planned delivery windows.

The Schedule Baseline and Cost Baseline are maintained as separate project management baselines. They must remain consistent with the approved scope and be managed through coordinated project change control.

### 1.3 Project Deliverables

The project comprises seven principal deliverables.

#### D1 — Digital Infrastructure

Establish the company's foundational digital infrastructure, including:

- Corporate domain registration and DNS configuration.
- Corporate email and collaboration workspace.
- Cloud document repository.
- Selection and configuration of appropriate hosting and cloud services.
- Identity and access management controls.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) for supported administrative and business accounts.
- Least-privilege access arrangements.
- Appropriate backup and recovery arrangements.
- Technical documentation and administrative ownership records.
- Baseline security and functional verification.

Google Workspace may be used for corporate email and collaboration, while AWS or another approved provider may be used for hosting and related cloud requirements, subject to the approved architecture, security requirements, and budget.

#### D2 — Corporate Web Presence

Design, implement, test, and publish a responsive bilingual corporate website in Arabic and English.

The website will include:

- Approved corporate information and service descriptions.
- A professional and responsive user interface.
- HTTPS configuration.
- Functional business inquiry and contact mechanisms.
- Basic browser compatibility and functional testing.
- Baseline security verification.
- Business-owner review and approval of published content.

Search-engine rankings, website traffic, customer conversion, and revenue are not guaranteed project outcomes.

#### D3 — Commercial and Legal Template Suite

Prepare five reusable commercial and legal document templates:

1. Commercial Proposal Template.
2. Statement of Work (SOW).
3. Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA).
4. Master Services Agreement (MSA).
5. Service Level Agreement (SLA).

The templates must be reviewed by relevant business stakeholders and, where appropriate, a qualified legal adviser familiar with applicable Omani requirements.

Review comments, unresolved legal issues, and required corrections must be documented before the templates are authorized for organizational use.

#### D4 — Operational Workspace

Establish a centralized Asana workspace to support project coordination and operational workflows.

The workspace will include:

- An agreed project and task hierarchy.
- Assigned responsibilities and workflow ownership.
- Task templates and appropriate custom fields.
- Milestone and progress tracking.
- Iterative delivery boards and backlogs where appropriate.
- Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) checklists.
- Appropriate access controls.
- Coordination with GitHub and the organization's documentation practices.

#### D5 — Digital Service Catalogue

Prepare and publish a digital service catalogue describing Bridge of Tomorrow's verified and approved service portfolio.

The catalogue will:

- Describe relevant service lines and intended SME client needs.
- Use activity descriptions verified against the company's current Commercial Registration.
- Include appropriate business contact channels.
- Identify service scope and exclusions where relevant.
- Include pricing information and service-level commitments only after the necessary business and legal approvals.
- Avoid claims that imply authorization for unverified or unlicensed activities.

#### D6 — Client Outreach Package

Prepare and dispatch five formal commercial proposals to qualified SME prospects in the agreed Muscat and South Al Batinah target areas.

The work includes:

- Identifying and qualifying suitable target businesses.
- Preparing customized proposals using the approved commercial template.
- Aligning proposals with verified service capabilities and approved commercial terms.
- Recording proposal references, dispatch dates, and follow-up status.
- Retaining appropriate dispatch evidence in a controlled project repository.
- Maintaining appropriate prospect information and communication records.

The objective is to complete and document five proposal dispatches. The project does not guarantee client responses, signed agreements, revenue, or commercial conversion.

#### D7 — Project Closeout Package

Complete the formal project closeout process, including:

- Deliverable acceptance review.
- Verification of completion criteria for D1–D6.
- Technical and operational asset handover.
- Documentation of outstanding issues and exceptions.
- Recording of follow-up actions and accountable owners.
- Final budget summary.
- Lessons Learned Retrospective.
- Preparation of the Project Closeout Report.
- Formal closure approval by the Project Sponsor or authorized delegate.

### 1.4 Out-of-Scope Activities

The following activities are excluded unless formally introduced through approved change control:

- Building construction or major renovation.
- Real estate acquisition.
- Permanent full-time employee recruitment during the planned eight-week execution window.
- Development of unrelated software products or large-scale enterprise applications.
- Production payment gateway implementation.
- Execution of regulated or otherwise unauthorized commercial activities.
- Import/export transactions beyond approved internal documentation and procedure-development activities.
- Guaranteed client conversion, executed contracts, revenue, or market share.
- Long-term managed IT services and continuous maintenance beyond agreed handover arrangements.
- Additional deliverables not included in the approved Scope Baseline.

No specific CR activity codes or confidential registration details are reproduced in this public document.

### 1.5 Project Assumptions

1. Bridge of Tomorrow maintains an active Commercial Registration, and relevant activity descriptions will be available for verification.
2. Required corporate content and brand assets will be provided in a timely manner.
3. Stakeholders will provide decisions within agreed review periods.
4. Review turnaround targets will be agreed with relevant stakeholders rather than assumed.
5. Required cloud, domain, collaboration, and repository services will be available on acceptable terms.
6. The Project Sponsor will make material decisions after formal designation.
7. Qualified external legal counsel will be available for the required review activities.
8. Required contributors can be engaged within authorized cost arrangements.
9. The selected architecture will remain proportionate to operational requirements and financial constraints.
10. The initial budget estimate will be reviewed before it is treated as an authorized Cost Baseline.
11. The eight-week duration will be validated against the actual working calendar and resource availability.

If an assumption proves incorrect and materially affects delivery, the Project Manager must assess the impact and document any required corrective action or change request.

### 1.6 Project Constraints

**Financial Constraint**

The preliminary planning estimate is USD 1,500–2,500, approximately OMR 570–960. This is an indicative estimate, not an approved spending limit.

The authorized spending limit, included cost categories, and delegated spending authority must be documented in the approved Budget Baseline.

**Schedule Constraint**

The planned duration is eight calendar weeks, nominally equivalent to 40 business days under a five-day working-week assumption.

Actual start and target completion dates must be confirmed. Public holidays, resource availability, supplier lead times, and external approval dependencies must be reflected in the detailed schedule.

**Regulatory and Security Constraint**

Project activities must comply with applicable Omani legal and regulatory requirements, including commercial registration, activity-specific licensing, privacy, taxation, contractual obligations, and information security, where applicable.

Passwords, API keys, private keys, access tokens, and other secrets must not be committed to public GitHub repositories.

**Resource Constraint**

The project will use confirmed internal responsibilities and appropriately engaged contractor arrangements. Permanent full-time recruitment during the planned execution window is excluded.

### 1.7 Key Project Dependencies

Project Genesis depends on:

- Formal approval of the Project Charter and required project baselines.
- Verification of current Commercial Registration information and relevant activity descriptions.
- Timely domain registration and configuration.
- Availability of approved cloud and collaboration services.
- Completion of required legal and commercial reviews.
- Approval of website and service catalogue content.
- Availability and engagement of relevant contributors.
- Timely stakeholder decisions and approvals.
- Availability of suitable target SME prospects.
- Appropriate authorization of external-facing service descriptions and commercial terms.

Dependencies must be monitored through the Schedule Baseline and project status reporting.

### 2.1 WBS Decomposition Rules

- Each lowest-level WBS item represents identifiable work with a defined output or completion condition.
- Work packages must support assignment, scheduling, monitoring, and acceptance.
- Detailed sequencing and day-to-day execution are maintained in the Schedule Baseline and Asana workspace.
- Each work package must have one designated Accountable owner.
- Responsibility assignments must remain consistent with GEN-STK-001.
- Changes to WBS structure or deliverables must follow formal change control.

**Sequencing Note:** WBS numbering represents hierarchical decomposition rather than chronological execution order. Website Quality & Security Testing (1.3.4) must be completed before Production Website Release (1.3.3).

### 3.1 Closeout Scheduling Principle

Acceptance review and asset handover are performed progressively as deliverables become available.

This permits:

- Early review of completed deliverables D1–D5.
- Preparation of asset records during implementation.
- Final review of D6 after proposal dispatch.
- Final handover confirmation before the closeout report is submitted.

The final closure report and Sponsor sign-off remain dependent on completion of acceptance review, handover, and lessons learned.

The planned Week 8 closeout sequence remains subject to detailed day-level scheduling and resource validation.

## 5. Scope Verification and Acceptance

### 5.1 Scope Verification

The Project Manager will coordinate scope verification throughout delivery.

Verification confirms that completed work meets the defined scope and acceptance criteria.

Evidence may include:

- Technical configuration checklists.
- Website testing results.
- Approved corporate content.
- Reviewed legal and commercial templates.
- Operational workspace review records.
- Published service catalogue.
- Proposal dispatch evidence.
- Asset handover records.
- Formal acceptance decisions.

### 5.2 Deliverable Acceptance

A deliverable is accepted only when:

1. Its defined acceptance criteria have been met.
2. Required verification evidence is available.
3. Outstanding defects and exceptions have been documented.
4. The designated authorized approver has recorded the acceptance decision.

A deliverable must not be considered accepted solely because it has been published, demonstrated, or marked complete in a task-management tool.

### 5.3 Scope Change Control

Any proposed change affecting project deliverables, boundaries, acceptance criteria, schedule, budget, quality, security, or regulatory obligations must be documented in a Change Request.

The Project Manager will:

1. Record the proposed change and rationale.
2. Assess impact on scope, schedule, cost, resources, quality, security, and compliance.
3. Identify relevant dependencies and alternatives.
4. Submit material changes to the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative.
5. Record the decision.
6. Update the relevant approved project documents when authorization is obtained.
7. Communicate approved changes to affected stakeholders.

No material scope change is considered approved solely because it has been discussed informally.

### 5.4 Traceability

Each work package must remain traceable across the project documentation and execution systems.

- Asana tasks and GitHub issues should reference their WBS codes.
- Deliverable evidence must identify the relevant deliverable ID (D1–D7).
- Acceptance evidence and decisions must be retained in controlled company records.
- Risks must be traceable to GEN-RSK-001.
- Responsibility assignments must remain consistent with GEN-STK-001.
- Schedule and cost impacts must be assessed through GEN-SCH-001 and GEN-BGT-001.
- Approved changes must be reflected in the relevant records.
