## 1. Project Scope Statement

### 1.1 Project Objective

The objective of Project Genesis is to establish the core digital infrastructure, corporate web presence, commercial and legal document templates, operational management workflows, and initial client outreach capabilities required to support an IT and business services enterprise operating in the Sultanate of Oman.

The project will establish a documented, secure, and operationally manageable business foundation within an eight-calendar-week delivery window, subject to the approved start date, budget, stakeholder availability, and external dependencies.

The project will use a hybrid delivery approach, combining structured governance and formal approval checkpoints with iterative implementation and testing of technical and operational deliverables.

The initial planning budget is estimated at USD 1,500–2,500. This estimate is provisional and must not be treated as an authorized expenditure ceiling until the Project Sponsor approves the cost baseline.

### 1.2 In-Scope Deliverables

The following deliverables establish the project scope baseline.

#### D1 — Digital Infrastructure

Establish the company's foundational digital infrastructure, including:

- Corporate domain registration and DNS configuration.
- Corporate email and cloud document workspace.
- Selection and configuration of appropriate hosting and cloud services.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) and least-privilege access controls for supported accounts.
- Baseline security, account ownership, and access-management procedures.
- Appropriate backup and recovery arrangements.
- Technical documentation and administrative handover records.

Google Workspace may be used for corporate email and collaboration, while AWS or another approved provider may be used for website hosting, subject to the approved architecture and budget.

An `.om` domain or secondary domain may be selected only after eligibility, availability, cost, and business requirements have been verified. A separate backup endpoint is not a mandatory baseline deliverable unless justified and approved.

#### D2 — Corporate Web Presence

Design, implement, test, and publish a responsive bilingual corporate website in Arabic and English.

The website will include:

- Approved corporate information and service descriptions.
- A professional, responsive user interface.
- HTTPS configuration.
- Functional business inquiry and contact mechanisms.
- Basic browser compatibility and functional testing.
- Baseline security verification.
- Business-owner approval of published content.

The website must accurately represent the company's verified service portfolio. Search-engine rankings, website traffic, customer conversion, and revenue are not guaranteed outcomes.

#### D3 — Commercial and Legal Template Suite

Prepare five reusable commercial and legal document templates:

1. Commercial Proposal Template.
2. Statement of Work (SOW).
3. Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA).
4. Master Services Agreement (MSA).
5. Service Level Agreement (SLA).

The templates must be reviewed by the relevant business stakeholders and, where appropriate, a qualified legal adviser familiar with applicable Omani requirements.

Required review comments and unresolved legal issues must be documented and addressed or explicitly escalated before the templates are approved for organizational use.

Approval of standard templates does not replace transaction-specific review or guarantee that every future contract will be suitable for every client engagement.

#### D4 — Operational Workspace

Establish a centralized Asana workspace to support project coordination and operational workflows.

The workspace will include:

- An agreed project and task hierarchy.
- Assigned responsibilities and workflow ownership.
- Task templates, custom fields, and milestone tracking where appropriate.
- Iterative delivery boards and backlogs where suitable for the delivery approach.
- Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) checklists.
- Appropriate access controls.
- Integration with the organization's documentation and version-control practices.

Scrum-specific ceremonies, roles, and practices will be used only where they are explicitly adopted for the relevant work.

#### D5 — Digital Service Catalogue

Prepare and publish a digital service catalogue describing the company's approved service portfolio.

The catalogue will:

- Describe relevant service lines and intended SME client needs.
- Use activity descriptions verified against the company's current Commercial Registration (CR).
- Include appropriate business contact channels.
- Identify service scope and exclusions where relevant.
- Include pricing information and service-level benchmarks only after the necessary business and legal approvals.
- Avoid claims that imply authorization for unverified or unlicensed activities.

The catalogue will be published on the corporate website or another approved digital channel.

The catalogue must not be published as a definitive representation of authorized activities until the relevant registration details have been verified.

#### D6 — Client Outreach Package

Prepare and dispatch five formal commercial proposals to qualified SME prospects in the agreed Muscat and South Al Batinah target areas.

The work includes:

- Identifying and qualifying suitable target businesses.
- Preparing customized proposals using the approved commercial template.
- Aligning proposals with verified service capabilities and approved commercial terms.
- Recording proposal references, dispatch dates, and follow-up status.
- Retaining dispatch evidence in an appropriately controlled project repository.

The objective is to complete and document five proposal dispatches. The project does not guarantee client responses, signed agreements, revenue, or commercial conversion.

#### D7 — Project Closeout Package

Complete the formal project closeout process, including:

- Deliverable acceptance review.
- Verification of the agreed completion criteria for D1–D6.
- Technical and operational asset handover.
- Documentation of outstanding issues, exceptions, and follow-up actions.
- Final budget summary.
- Lessons Learned Retrospective.
- Preparation of the Project Closeout Report.
- Formal closure approval by the Project Sponsor or authorized delegate.

Any uncompleted deliverable or accepted exception must be documented, assigned an accountable owner, and accompanied by an agreed follow-up action.

### 1.3 Out-of-Scope Activities

The following activities are excluded from the approved baseline unless authorized through formal change control.

- Construction of residential or non-residential buildings, including activities associated with CR code 410001.
- Acquisition of physical real estate or facilities.
- Permanent full-time employee recruitment during the eight-week project execution window.
- Custom software products or large-scale enterprise applications outside the approved corporate website scope.
- Live production payment gateway implementation.
- Execution of regulated or otherwise unauthorized commercial activities.
- Import/export transactions beyond the approved operational documentation and procedures.
- Guaranteed client conversion, executed contracts, revenue, or market share.
- Long-term managed IT services and continuous maintenance beyond the agreed handover arrangements.
- Additional deliverables that have not been approved through change control.

Scope-related risks and proposed changes must be managed in accordance with GEN-RSK-001, including RSK-003 — Scope Creep.

### 1.4 Project Assumptions

The scope and delivery plan are based on the following assumptions:

- Current Commercial Registration details and relevant company information will be made available for verification.
- Required corporate content and brand assets will be provided in a timely manner.
- Stakeholders will provide decisions within agreed review periods.
- A target turnaround of 48 hours for critical-path reviews will be adopted only where agreed with the relevant stakeholders.
- Required cloud, domain, collaboration, and repository services will be available on acceptable terms, subject to provider conditions and technical suitability.
- The Project Sponsor will make material scope, budget, and schedule decisions promptly.
- Qualified external legal counsel will be available for the agreed review activities.
- Required contractors and contributors can be engaged within the approved cost baseline.
- The selected architecture will remain proportionate to the project's operational requirements and financial constraints.

If an assumption proves incorrect and materially affects delivery, the Lead Project Manager must assess the impact and document any required corrective action or change request.

### 1.5 Project Constraints

#### Financial Constraint

The current planning estimate is USD 1,500–2,500, approximately OMR 570–960.

The authorized spending limit, included cost categories, and delegated spending authority must be documented in the approved cost baseline.

Contractor fees, legal expenses, recurring subscriptions, infrastructure costs, administrative charges, and contingency must be explicitly considered.

No expenditure beyond the approved authority may be committed without the required approval.

#### Schedule Constraint

The planned duration is eight calendar weeks, nominally equivalent to 40 business days under a five-day working-week assumption.

The actual baseline start and target completion dates must be confirmed. Public holidays, contributor availability, and external approval dependencies must be reflected in the detailed schedule where applicable.

#### Regulatory and Security Constraint

Project activities must comply with applicable Omani legal and regulatory requirements.

Relevant requirements may include the Personal Data Protection Law issued under Royal Decree 6/2022, as amended by Royal Decree 68/2026, applicable executive regulations, commercial registration requirements, and activity-specific licensing obligations.

The applicability and implementation of these requirements must be verified with the relevant authorities or qualified legal advisers as appropriate.

Passwords, API keys, private keys, access tokens, and other secrets must not be committed to GitHub repositories.

#### Resource Constraint

The project will use the approved project team and contractor arrangements. Permanent full-time recruitment is excluded during the execution window.

Material resource gaps that threaten delivery must be escalated through project governance.

### 1.6 Key Dependencies

The project depends on:

- Formal approval of the Project Charter and project baseline.
- Verification of current Commercial Registration information and relevant activity codes.
- Timely domain registration and configuration.
- Availability of approved cloud and collaboration services.
- Completion of required legal and commercial reviews.
- Timely approval of website and service catalogue content.
- Availability of contributors for development, testing, and handover.
- Access to suitable target SME prospects for the outreach campaign.

Dependencies must be monitored through the project schedule and status reporting process.

### 2.1 WBS Decomposition Rules

- Each lowest-level WBS item must represent identifiable work with a defined output or completion condition.
- Work must be sufficiently decomposed to support assignment, scheduling, monitoring, and acceptance.
- The WBS describes the scope of work; detailed task sequencing and day-to-day execution will be managed in the project schedule and Asana workspace.
- Work packages must remain traceable to the relevant project deliverable.
- Any proposed addition, removal, or material change to the approved WBS must follow the project's change-control procedure.

### 3.1 Responsibility Definitions

- **Responsible:** The role or individual performing the work.
- **Accountable:** The single role or individual answerable for completion and acceptance of the work package.
- **Consulted:** Stakeholders whose specialist advice or review is required.
- **Informed:** Stakeholders who receive relevant status or decision updates.

Consulted and informed stakeholders should be identified in the Stakeholder and RACI Register (GEN-STK-001).

The accountable owner must be confirmed before the relevant work package is authorized.

### 3.2 Acceptance and Quality Rules

- Acceptance criteria must be verifiable through documented evidence.
- Completed work must be reviewed by the appropriate designated authority.
- Rejected deliverables must have documented reasons and corrective actions.
- Material defects or unresolved exceptions must be recorded in the project issue register.
- Legal review must not be represented as a blanket certification of regulatory compliance.
- Formal acceptance of a deliverable must be recorded in the approved project workspace or document repository.

## 5. Governance and Scope Control

### 5.1 Baseline Change Control

The approved Scope Statement, WBS, WBS Dictionary, schedule, and cost baseline constitute the project's scope and planning baseline.

Any proposed change affecting scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria, schedule, budget, security, or regulatory obligations must be documented in a Change Request.

The Lead Project Manager will:

1. Record the proposed change and its rationale.
2. Assess the impact on scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, security, and compliance.
3. Identify dependencies and alternative approaches where appropriate.
4. Submit material changes to the Project Sponsor for approval.
5. Record the decision and update the applicable baseline documents when approved.

No material change is considered approved until the authorized decision has been recorded.

### 5.2 Work Authorization

Before a work package begins, the following must be clear:

- Responsible role and accountable owner.
- Required inputs and dependencies.
- Planned delivery window.
- Applicable acceptance criteria.
- Required approvals and budget authority.

Work packages that depend on legal, regulatory, or other formal approval must not proceed beyond the authorized boundary while that approval remains outstanding.

### 5.3 Traceability

Each work package must remain traceable across project documentation and execution systems.

- Asana tasks and GitHub issues should reference the corresponding WBS code, for example, `[WBS 1.3.2]`.
- Deliverable documentation must identify the relevant deliverable ID (D1–D7).
- Acceptance evidence and material decisions must be retained in an appropriately controlled repository.
- The Risk Register (GEN-RSK-001) must remain linked to the scope and schedule baselines where relevant.

### 5.4 Scope Verification

The Lead Project Manager will coordinate scope verification throughout delivery and conduct a formal acceptance review during project closeout.

Scope verification confirms that the completed work meets the agreed scope and acceptance criteria. It does not, by itself, constitute a separate legal certification, guarantee of commercial results, or confirmation that excluded activities have been authorized.
