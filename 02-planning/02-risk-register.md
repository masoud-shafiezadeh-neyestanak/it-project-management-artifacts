## 1. Executive Summary

This Risk Register identifies, assesses, monitors, and supports the management of threats that could affect the successful delivery of Project Genesis, a real business and operational readiness initiative undertaken within Bridge of Tomorrow, an Oman-registered company with an active Commercial Registration (CR).

The project aims to establish and strengthen the company's digital infrastructure, corporate web presence, commercial documentation, operational workflows, digital service catalogue, and initial client outreach capabilities.

The register covers technical, cybersecurity, legal and regulatory, financial, contractual, operational, schedule, resource, and market-related risks.

Project Genesis uses a tailored qualitative Probability–Impact assessment method appropriate to its scope, planned duration, preliminary budget, and resource model.

The initial register identifies eleven principal risks. Their probability and impact ratings are preliminary estimates and must be reviewed against actual project conditions, supplier quotations, stakeholder decisions, technical findings, and qualified regulatory or legal advice.

The Project Manager is responsible for maintaining this register, coordinating mitigation actions, monitoring risk exposure, and escalating material concerns to the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative.

Until relevant functional resources are formally assigned, the Project Manager will retain interim accountability for tracking the identified risks. This does not mean that the Project Manager must personally perform every specialist mitigation activity.

Priority attention is required for:

- Personal data protection and applicable regulatory requirements.
- Cybersecurity, credential management, and organizational ownership of digital assets.
- Budget uncertainty and external contractor dependencies.
- Verification of the company's registered activities and any additional authorization requirements.
- Timely approval of technical, legal, commercial, and business deliverables.
- Availability of suitably qualified project contributors.

A risk is not considered closed merely because a mitigation action has been planned or completed. Closure requires evidence that the risk has been resolved, is no longer relevant, or has been formally accepted by an authorized decision-maker.

## 2. Risk Assessment Methodology

### 2.1 Probability Scale

Probability represents the estimated likelihood that a risk event will occur during the project or the relevant initial operational period.

| Rating | Classification | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare | Very unlikely to occur. |
| 2 | Unlikely | Possible, but not expected under normal conditions. |
| 3 | Possible | Could reasonably occur. |
| 4 | Likely | Expected to occur under current conditions. |
| 5 | Almost Certain | Expected to occur unless conditions change. |

### 2.2 Impact Scale

Impact represents the severity of the consequences for project objectives, cost, schedule, quality, security, business continuity, or legal and regulatory compliance.

| Rating | Classification | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Negligible | Minimal disruption with no material effect on project objectives. |
| 2 | Minor | Limited rework or disruption manageable within normal task execution. |
| 3 | Moderate | Noticeable delay, additional cost, or rework requiring Project Manager intervention. |
| 4 | Major | Material threat to a milestone, authorized budget, service availability, or a critical deliverable. |
| 5 | Critical | Potential serious legal or regulatory consequences, major security or data incident, critical data loss, or threat to project viability. |

Impact must be assessed using the most significant reasonably foreseeable consequence.

A legal, regulatory, privacy, or cybersecurity risk may therefore receive an impact rating of 5 even when its estimated probability is relatively low.

### 2.3 Risk Score Calculation

The initial risk score is calculated using:

**Risk Score = Probability × Impact**

| Score | Rating | Required Management Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1–6 | Low | Monitor and implement proportionate controls. |
| 8–12 | Medium | Assign mitigation actions and review regularly. |
| 15–25 | High | Prioritize mitigation, assign clear deadlines, and report to the Project Sponsor. |

The thresholds are consistent with the possible products of integer probability and impact ratings from 1 to 5.

The score is a prioritization tool and does not replace professional judgment. Material legal, regulatory, privacy, or security concerns must be escalated when necessary, regardless of their numerical score.

### 2.4 Risk Response Strategies

The following risk response strategies will be used:

| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| Avoid | Change the project approach to eliminate the risk exposure. |
| Mitigate | Reduce the likelihood of occurrence, impact, or both through appropriate controls. |
| Transfer / Share | Allocate an appropriate portion of the exposure to a qualified third party through suitable contractual or operational arrangements. |
| Accept | Acknowledge the risk and monitor it where the exposure is within approved tolerance. |
| Escalate | Refer the risk to the Project Sponsor or another authorized decision-maker when it exceeds the Project Manager's delegated authority or risk tolerance. |

Risk acceptance must be documented when the exposure is material or exceeds normal operational tolerance.

### 3.1 Risk Ownership Principle

Each risk must have exactly one accountable Risk Owner.

The Risk Owner is responsible for:

- Monitoring the risk.
- Coordinating relevant mitigation actions.
- Reviewing triggers and changes in exposure.
- Maintaining accurate risk status.
- Reporting material developments.
- Escalating risks that exceed delegated authority.

Supporting contributors may perform mitigation activities, but accountability for tracking and reporting remains with the designated Risk Owner.

When a suitable technical, marketing, or specialist resource is formally appointed, the Project Manager may recommend reassignment of the relevant risk ownership.

Any change must be recorded in the revision history and communicated to affected stakeholders.

## 4. Risk Mitigation and Action Plans

### RSK-001 — Cloud Architecture and Configuration

**Category:** Technical / Cloud

**Initial Exposure:** 2 × 4 = 8 (Medium)

**Risk Statement:**

The selected cloud, collaboration, hosting, or identity configuration may introduce implementation delays, misconfiguration, unnecessary complexity, or avoidable operating costs.

**Mitigation Actions:**

- Confirm and document the target architecture before production deployment.
- Use Google Workspace for corporate collaboration and document management where appropriate.
- Use AWS or another approved provider for website hosting only where justified by technical requirements, security, and budget.
- Configure least-privilege access and multi-factor authentication (MFA) for supported accounts.
- Validate DNS, HTTPS, hosting, access permissions, and required service integrations.
- Document backup, recovery, administrative access, and service ownership.
- Estimate and monitor cloud resource consumption.
- Apply infrastructure-as-code tooling only where it provides a clear benefit for the selected architecture.
- Avoid unnecessary infrastructure components and services.

**Risk Trigger / Early Warning:**

Unresolved architecture decisions, repeated configuration failures, unexpected vendor charges, unnecessary service complexity, or delays in environment readiness.

**Risk Owner:** Project Manager (interim).

**Supporting Roles:** Technical Contributor, once formally assigned; relevant service providers.

**Target:** Architecture agreed before production configuration; verification completed before website launch.

**Residual Risk:** To be reassessed after implementation and testing.

---

### RSK-002 — Commercial Registration Activities and Regulatory Requirements

**Category:** Legal / Regulatory

**Initial Exposure:** 2 × 5 = 10 (Medium)

**Risk Statement:**

Incorrect or outdated assumptions about Bridge of Tomorrow's registered commercial activities, additional licensing requirements, tax obligations, or other applicable Omani requirements may result in compliance gaps, delivery delays, or restrictions on planned services.

**Mitigation Actions:**

- Verify the latest official Commercial Registration information and exact registered activity descriptions.
- Confirm whether each planned service requires additional registration, authorization, or licensing.
- Map the proposed service catalogue against verified and authorized business activities.
- Engage a qualified External Legal Adviser where legal interpretation is required.
- Identify applicable tax and administrative obligations.
- Maintain a compliance action list with an accountable owner and target date.
- Store registration documents and other sensitive business records in an access-controlled location.
- Do not publish sensitive registration details in the public GitHub repository.
- Ensure that public website content and commercial proposals describe only verified and authorized services.

**Risk Trigger / Early Warning:**

Unverified activity descriptions, contradictory registration information, missing approvals, or legal advice identifying a licensing or authorization gap.

**Risk Owner:** Project Manager.

**Supporting Roles:** Authorized Company Representative, once confirmed; External Legal Adviser, when engaged.

**Target:** Verification completed before publication of definitive service claims or commencement of an activity requiring additional authorization.

**Residual Risk:** To be reassessed after registration verification and relevant legal review.

### RSK-003 — Scope Creep

**Category:** Operational / Scope

**Initial Exposure:** 3 × 4 = 12 (Medium)

**Risk Statement:**

Additional requirements may be introduced without formal evaluation, resulting in schedule delays, budget pressure, reduced quality, or incomplete delivery of the agreed project objectives.

**Mitigation Actions:**

- Use the current Project Charter (GEN-CH-001) and Scope Baseline (GEN-SBL-001) as the principal reference documents for scope management.
- Distinguish reviewed draft documents from formally approved project baselines.
- Require documented Change Requests for additions affecting scope, schedule, budget, security, quality, or regulatory compliance.
- Assess delivery impact before recommending approval.
- Obtain authorization from the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative for material changes.
- Record approved and rejected changes in the project change log.
- Keep excluded activities, including construction and unapproved payment gateway implementation, outside the project scope unless formally authorized.
- Maintain traceability between deliverables, WBS work packages, and execution tasks.

**Risk Trigger / Early Warning:**

New deliverables appearing in the backlog without approval, unbudgeted requests, repeated changes to acceptance criteria, or activities being undertaken outside the reviewed project scope.

**Risk Owner:** Project Manager.

**Supporting Roles:** Project Sponsor / Authorized Company Representative and relevant delivery contributors.

**Target:** Continuous throughout the project.

**Residual Risk:** To be reassessed during weekly reviews.

### RSK-004 — Budget Overrun

**Category:** Financial

**Initial Exposure:** 3 × 4 = 12 (Medium)

**Risk Statement:**

The preliminary budget estimate of USD 1,500–2,500 may be insufficient if contractor fees, legal review, recurring subscriptions, infrastructure consumption, taxes, or other administrative expenses are not fully identified and accounted for.

**Mitigation Actions:**

- Complete a detailed Budget Baseline before treating the estimate as an authorized spending limit.
- Obtain vendor and contractor quotations wherever practicable.
- Separate one-time costs, recurring subscriptions, contractor fees, legal expenses, applicable taxes, and contingency.
- Estimate cloud consumption based on the intended configuration and expected usage.
- Configure appropriate budget alerts and spending controls with cloud providers.
- Track actual expenditure, commitments, and forecast costs regularly.
- Require the appropriate company approval before committing expenditure beyond delegated limits or the formally approved budget.
- Identify proportionate lower-cost alternatives if forecasts exceed the authorized budget.
- Avoid creating or retaining unnecessary paid infrastructure or subscriptions.

**Risk Trigger / Early Warning:**

Forecast expenditure exceeds the approved budget, material costs remain unquoted, recurring subscription costs are omitted, or an unapproved financial commitment is proposed.

**Risk Owner:** Project Manager (interim).

**Supporting Roles:** Project Sponsor / Authorized Company Representative and relevant contributors.

**Target:** Detailed cost baseline reviewed before significant commitments; expenditure monitored weekly throughout delivery.

**Residual Risk:** To be reassessed after quotations are confirmed and the Budget Baseline is authorized.

### RSK-005 — Contractual and Service Governance Disputes

**Category:** Commercial / Contractual

**Initial Exposure:** 2 × 4 = 8 (Medium)

**Risk Statement:**

Ambiguous service descriptions, unclear deliverables, unrealistic service-level commitments, or inadequately reviewed commercial and legal templates may lead to client disputes or obligations that Bridge of Tomorrow cannot reasonably meet.

**Mitigation Actions:**

- Require appropriate internal approval before committing to non-standard commercial or service terms.
- Document client-specific variations.
- Ensure that proposed services are consistent with verified commercial activities.
- Maintain version control and restrict unauthorized changes to approved templates.

**Risk Trigger / Early Warning:**

Unclear scope statements, conflicting contractual clauses, unrealistic response-time commitments, inaccurate service descriptions, or unresolved legal review comments.

**Risk Owner:** Project Manager.

**Supporting Roles:** External Legal Adviser, once engaged; Authorized Company Signatory, once confirmed.

**Target:** Required templates reviewed and authorized before formal proposals or contractual commitments rely on them.

**Residual Risk:** To be reassessed after legal review and internal authorization.

### RSK-007 — Personal Data Protection and Regulatory Compliance

**Category:** Privacy / Regulatory

**Initial Exposure:** 3 × 5 = 15 (High)

**Risk Statement:**

Project Genesis may fail to identify or implement applicable personal data protection requirements affecting website inquiries, business contacts, client records, cloud processing, or commercial outreach.

**Mitigation Actions:**

- Obtain a qualified review of applicable Omani personal data protection requirements, including Royal Decree 6/2022, subsequent amendments, and applicable executive regulations.
- Identify the categories of personal data collected, the purposes of processing, and the systems or providers handling the data.
- Determine applicable requirements for privacy notices, lawful processing, consent where required, retention, deletion, and data subject requests.
- Review third-party cloud processing arrangements and relevant cross-border data processing considerations.
- Review requirements applicable to commercial marketing communications before client outreach begins.
- Restrict access to customer and prospect information.
- Document appropriate retention and deletion procedures.
- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal data during the initial launch.
- Determine whether the appointment of a Data Protection Officer or engagement of a privacy specialist is required under the applicable legal framework.
- Record privacy reviews and required corrective actions.

**Risk Trigger / Early Warning:**

Personal data collection begins before the required review, uncertain processing permissions, missing privacy information, unresolved advice concerning cloud or marketing activities, or uncertainty about a required DPO appointment.

**Risk Owner:** Project Manager (interim).

**Supporting Roles:** External Legal Adviser; Technical Contributor, once assigned; DPO or privacy specialist where required and formally designated.

**Target:** Applicable requirements assessed before relevant personal data processing or commercial outreach begins.

**Escalation:** Material privacy concerns or possible compliance violations must be escalated promptly to the authorized company representative.

**Residual Risk:** To be reassessed after legal review and implementation of required controls.

### RSK-008 — Cybersecurity and Credential Exposure

**Category:** Cybersecurity

**Initial Exposure:** 3 × 5 = 15 (High)

**Risk Statement:**

Unauthorized access, exposed credentials, inadequate permissions, insecure configurations, or data loss may compromise company information, digital assets, or operational services.

**Mitigation Actions:**

- Keep administrative accounts, business assets, and repositories under organizational control.
- Enable MFA wherever supported, particularly for privileged accounts.
- Apply least-privilege access and remove unnecessary permissions.
- Use a secure credential manager and approved secret storage.
- Never commit passwords, API keys, private keys, access tokens, or other secrets to GitHub.
- Restrict repository and cloud administration privileges to authorized personnel.
- Configure backups and verify recovery procedures.
- Perform baseline security and configuration checks before production launch.
- Revoke access promptly when contractors or contributors no longer require it.
- Maintain an inventory of critical digital assets and their administrative owners.
- Document the process for reporting and responding to suspected security incidents.

**Risk Trigger / Early Warning:**

Exposed credentials, unexpected administrative activity, failed access-control tests, unpatched critical vulnerabilities, unverified backups, or unauthorized access attempts.

**Risk Owner:** Project Manager (interim).

**Supporting Roles:** Technical Contributor, once assigned; relevant service providers.

**Target:** Baseline controls verified before production launch; access and backup arrangements reviewed regularly.

**Escalation:** Suspected material security incidents or significant exposure of sensitive information must be escalated immediately through the appropriate company incident-management channels.

**Residual Risk:** To be reassessed after controls are implemented and verified.

Critical assets held in personal accounts, missing source code or documentation, disputed ownership, refusal or inability to transfer access, or reliance on undocumented contractor knowledge.

**Target:** Ownership and handover requirements established before relevant contractor engagement; transfer verified before project closeout.

## 5. Risk Monitoring and Governance

### 5.1 Review Frequency

The Project Manager will review the Risk Register at least weekly as part of the project status review.

High risks and risks with material changes in probability or impact must receive additional attention when required.

A review must also be initiated when:

- A risk trigger occurs.
- A material scope, schedule, or budget change is proposed.
- A legal or regulatory requirement is identified or changes.
- A security or data incident is suspected.
- A major dependency becomes unavailable.
- A mitigation action fails or becomes overdue.
- A specialist contributor or external adviser is engaged and risk ownership needs reassessment.
- The Project Sponsor or authorized company representative changes.

### 5.2 Escalation Criteria

A risk must be escalated to the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative when:

- Its initial or updated score is 15 or higher.
- Its potential impact exceeds the Project Manager's delegated authority.
- It threatens a critical milestone or an approved budget baseline.
- It presents a material legal, regulatory, privacy, or cybersecurity concern.
- A major mitigation decision requires additional funding, a scope change, or formal company authorization.
- The Project Manager cannot resolve the exposure within the assigned responsibilities and available resources.

Material security incidents and possible serious compliance violations must be reported promptly and must not be delayed merely because a formal risk score has not yet been calculated.

Until the Project Sponsor has been formally designated, matters requiring Sponsor-level decisions must be escalated to the authorized company representative under the company's existing authority arrangements.

All escalations and resulting decisions must be recorded.

### 5.3 Risk Status Definitions

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Open | Risk identified and recorded; further action or monitoring is required. |
| In Progress | Mitigation activities are underway. |
| Monitoring | Controls have been implemented and the remaining exposure is being monitored. |
| Closed | The risk is no longer applicable, has been resolved, or has been formally accepted and closed by the authorized decision-maker. |

A risk must not be marked Closed solely because a planned mitigation action has been completed.

### 5.4 Residual Risk Assessment

Residual risk is the remaining exposure after mitigation controls have been implemented and their effectiveness assessed.

Residual probability, impact, and score must be recorded during subsequent reviews when sufficient evidence is available.

Until the effectiveness of a mitigation has been verified, the initial score remains the documented baseline for that risk.

Where residual risk remains High, the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative must decide whether further treatment, schedule or scope changes, or formal risk acceptance is appropriate.

### 5.5 Risk Action Tracking

The Project Manager must maintain action records for material mitigation activities.

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Risk ID | Reference to the corresponding entry in the Risk Log |
| Action Description | Specific mitigation or corrective action |
| Action Owner | Individual responsible for completing the action |
| Target Date | Agreed completion date |
| Action Status | Not Started, In Progress, Blocked, or Completed |
| Evidence | Link to the relevant test, approval, document, or other evidence |
| Residual Assessment | Updated assessment after implementation, where appropriate |

Actions must be specific, verifiable, proportionate to the risk exposure, and assigned to an appropriately qualified resource.

### 5.6 Risk Ownership Reassessment

Risk ownership will be reviewed when relevant specialist resources are formally engaged.

Potential reassignment areas include:

- Technical and cloud-related risks (RSK-001, RSK-006, and RSK-008).
- Privacy and data protection risks (RSK-007).
- Market and client outreach risks (RSK-011).

The Project Manager retains oversight of the overall Risk Register even when individual mitigation actions or risk ownership responsibilities are assigned to other contributors.

Any change to Risk Owners must be documented in the register and communicated to affected stakeholders.
