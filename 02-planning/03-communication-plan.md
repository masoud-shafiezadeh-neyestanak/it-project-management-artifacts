## 1. Executive Summary & Governance Objectives

### 1.1 Purpose

The Project Communication Management Plan defines the communication framework, stakeholder information requirements, communication channels, reporting formats, meeting practices, and escalation procedures necessary to support the successful delivery of Project Genesis.

Its primary purpose is to ensure that the right information reaches the right stakeholders through appropriate channels, at the appropriate time, and in a format that supports timely decisions and effective project execution.

The plan establishes a consistent communication approach across project governance, technical implementation, operational coordination, legal review, and client engagement.

### 1.2 Communication Objectives

The communication management approach aims to:

- Maintain transparency regarding project progress, deliverables, schedule, and budget.
- Ensure timely communication between the Lead Project Manager, delivery contributors, Project Sponsor, and external advisers.
- Provide consistent and concise executive reporting.
- Support coordination across Asana, GitHub, and Google Workspace.
- Maintain traceable records of decisions, approvals, issues, and action items.
- Ensure that material risks, blockers, and deviations are escalated to the appropriate decision-makers.
- Protect confidential business information and personal data.
- Support effective communication with external SME prospects and other relevant stakeholders.

### 1.3 Communication Principles

The following principles govern project communications.

**1. Audience-Appropriate Communication**

Information must be tailored to the audience's responsibilities, decision-making authority, and information requirements.

Executive communications should focus on project status, material risks, required decisions, and business impact. Technical communications should include sufficient detail to support implementation and verification.

**2. TL;DR Reporting Principle**

Executive status reports, significant escalation messages, and decision requests should begin with a concise two-to-three-sentence summary identifying:

- Overall situation or project status.
- Key issue or decision required.
- Relevant deadline or requested action.

Detailed supporting information should follow the summary where necessary.

**3. RAG Status Reporting**

Red, Amber, and Green indicators will be used consistently in weekly reports and milestone reviews.

| Status | Definition | Management Response |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 Green — On Track | Work is progressing within approved scope, schedule, and cost baselines, with no unresolved critical concern. | Continue planned execution and routine monitoring. |
| 🟡 Amber — At Risk | A potential deviation, dependency, resource constraint, or material issue exists but can be managed within delegated authority. | Assign corrective actions, monitor closely, and report to the Lead Project Manager. |
| 🔴 Red — Critical | An approved baseline is forecast to be breached, a high-priority risk or serious incident requires intervention, or a decision beyond delegated authority is necessary. | Escalate promptly to the Project Sponsor and initiate an appropriate response. |

**4. 5W1H Communication Alignment**

Each planned communication event should identify:

- **Who:** Intended audience and responsible owner.
- **What:** Information, update, or decision being communicated.
- **When:** Frequency, timing, or triggering event.
- **Where:** Approved communication channel.
- **Why:** Purpose and expected outcome.
- **How:** Format, supporting evidence, and record location.

**5. Transparency and Traceability**

Material decisions, changes, approvals, and commitments must be documented in the appropriate project record.

Informal messages or verbal discussions do not replace required formal approvals.

**6. Information Security**

Confidential information, personal data, credentials, and security-sensitive material must be handled through approved channels and according to organizational access and information-handling requirements.

### 2.1 Communication Timing and Response Expectations

The following are proposed operational communication targets, subject to stakeholder agreement:

- Routine internal messages should normally receive an acknowledgment or response within two business days.
- Critical blockers must be reported as soon as identified rather than waiting for the next scheduled meeting.
- Formal meeting notes and action items should normally be published within one business day.
- Material decisions must be documented within one business day of being made.
- Time-critical incidents and regulatory matters must follow their applicable escalation and notification requirements without waiting for normal reporting cycles.

The meeting days and times in this plan are proposed arrangements and must be confirmed during project mobilization.

### 2.2 Communication Language

- Internal project documentation, governance reports, and technical records: English.
- Corporate website and customer-facing service materials: Arabic and English.
- External client communications: Arabic or English according to the recipient's preference and business requirements.
- Formal legal and regulatory communications: The language and format required by the relevant authority or authorized adviser.

## 3. Communication Channel Stack & Tooling Hierarchy

To minimize information fragmentation and unnecessary communication overhead, Project Genesis will use the following tool hierarchy.

```text
[ GitHub ] ------------> Controlled Documentation & Technical Records
                          Source Code, Architecture, Technical SOPs,
                          Approved Markdown Governance Documents

[ Asana Workspace ] ---> Operational Execution & Coordination
                          Tasks, Owners, Dependencies, Milestones,
                          Checklists, and Delivery Status

[ Google Workspace ] --> Formal Business Communication & Documents
                          Corporate Email, Restricted Drive,
                          Legal Reviews, Commercial Proposals,
                          Formal Approval Records

[ Secure Messaging ] --> Time-Sensitive Operational Coordination
                          Urgent Blockers, Short Updates,
                          Immediate Coordination
                          No Binding Approvals or Secrets
```

### 3.1 Channel Usage Guidelines

**1. GitHub Repository**

GitHub serves as the version-controlled repository for:

- Source code and technical documentation.
- Architecture descriptions and implementation records.
- Technical SOPs and configuration documentation.
- Approved, non-sensitive Markdown project documents.
- Change records and relevant technical decisions.

Repository access must follow organizational security policies.

Public repositories must not contain confidential company information, client personal data, private contractual documents, sensitive registration details, credentials, or other restricted information.

GitHub is not the authoritative repository for signed legal originals or confidential commercial records unless an appropriately controlled private environment has been explicitly approved.

**2. Asana Workspace**

The organization-controlled Asana workspace serves as the operational coordination platform.

It is used for:

- Task management and assignment.
- Work-package tracking and WBS references.
- Dependencies and blockers.
- Milestone progress.
- Action-item tracking.
- Operational checklists and workflow management.

Every relevant task should reference its corresponding WBS code, such as `[WBS 1.3.2]`, and identify the responsible and accountable roles.

**3. Google Workspace**

Google Workspace is used for formal corporate communication and restricted business documentation, including:

- Official corporate emails.
- Executive status reporting.
- Legal template reviews.
- Commercial proposal preparation and dispatch.
- Controlled business documents.
- Formal approvals and supporting records.

Access to restricted documents must be granted only to authorized individuals.

**4. Approved Secure Messaging**

Approved messaging channels may be used for rapid operational coordination during agreed business hours.

They must not be treated as the authoritative record for formal approvals, contractual commitments, or sensitive information.

Material decisions made through messaging must be formally documented and linked to the relevant project record within one business day.

### 3.2 Record Ownership and Source of Truth

| Information Type | Authoritative Record |
|---|---|
| Project Charter and Planning Baselines | Controlled GitHub Repository or approved restricted document repository |
| Operational Tasks and Task Status | Asana |
| Source Code and Technical Changes | GitHub Repository |
| Formal Legal Documents | Restricted Google Drive or approved legal document repository |
| Commercial Proposals | Restricted Corporate Document Repository |
| Formal Approvals | Documented Approval Records |
| Issues and Risks | Relevant Project Issue Log and Risk Register |
| Project Decisions and Change Requests | Project Decision Log and Change Control Records |
| Security Incidents | Restricted Incident Register |

Where two systems contain conflicting information, the Lead Project Manager must identify the authoritative record, resolve the discrepancy, and document any required correction.

## 4. Meeting Management & Documentation

### 4.1 Meeting Preparation

Meetings should be organized according to their purpose and should involve only the stakeholders required to contribute, review, or make decisions.

Where appropriate:

- Agendas should be shared before scheduled meetings.
- Participants should receive relevant documents in advance.
- Required decisions and expected outcomes should be identified.
- Meeting duration should be proportionate to the subject and complexity.

Daily coordination should be asynchronous by default, with synchronous meetings arranged when necessary.

### 4.2 Meeting Minutes and Action Tracking

The meeting owner or designated note-taker must document material discussions and decisions.

Meeting records should contain:

- Meeting title and date.
- Participants and relevant roles.
- Key discussion points.
- Decisions and approval outcomes.
- Identified issues and dependencies.
- Assigned action items.
- Action owners and target completion dates.

Meeting notes and action records should normally be published within one business day.

### 4.3 Decision Documentation

Material decisions must be recorded in the Project Decision Log or the appropriate formal approval record.

Each decision should include:

- Decision reference.
- Date and decision authority.
- Description and rationale.
- Affected scope, schedule, budget, or deliverable.
- Required follow-up action.
- Relevant document or approval reference.

A decision communicated informally must not be treated as formal authorization until the required approval is recorded.

## 5. Escalation Framework & Decision Authority

The escalation framework establishes how operational issues, schedule deviations, budget concerns, and material risks move through the project governance structure.

### 5.1 Escalation Levels

**Level 1 — Operational / Task Owner**

Responsible roles: Task Owners, Technical Contributors, Marketing Lead, and other assigned delivery contributors.

Decision authority:

- Day-to-day task execution.
- Routine technical and operational adjustments.
- Resolution of minor blockers within approved work-package boundaries.
- Internal coordination and corrective actions.

Escalation triggers:

- A task blocker remains unresolved for more than one business day.
- A technical dependency prevents expected progress.
- A task is forecast to miss its agreed target.
- An issue requires coordination beyond the task owner's authority.

The Task Owner must document the issue and notify the Lead Project Manager when escalation is required.

**Level 2 — Project Management / Lead Project Manager**

Responsible authority: Lead Project Manager.

Decision authority:

- Task resequencing within the approved baseline.
- Coordination of resources within delegated authority.
- Monitoring and execution of risk mitigation actions.
- Vendor follow-up and operational coordination.
- Review of schedule and cost forecasts.
- Recommendation of corrective actions and change requests.

Escalation triggers to Level 3:

- A forecast schedule variance exceeds five business days or threatens the approved project completion date.
- Forecast expenditure exceeds the approved budget or delegated spending authority.
- A material scope change is required.
- A risk receives a High rating (score of 15 or higher).
- A significant legal, regulatory, privacy, or security concern is identified.
- A decision exceeds the Lead Project Manager's delegated authority.

**Level 3 — Project Governance / Project Sponsor**

Responsible authority: Project Sponsor or formally authorized executive representative.

Decision authority:

- Approval of material changes to project scope and objectives.
- Approval of changes to the authorized budget baseline.
- Approval of material schedule baseline revisions.
- Resolution of escalated stakeholder conflicts.
- Acceptance or treatment decisions concerning material residual risks.
- Decisions concerning project suspension or termination.
- Authorization of formal external communications where required.

High risks and serious legal, privacy, or cybersecurity concerns must be escalated promptly, regardless of whether a schedule or financial threshold has been exceeded.

### 5.2 RAG and Escalation Alignment

| Condition | Reporting Status | Required Action |
|---|---|---|
| Progress within approved baselines; no unresolved critical concern | Green | Routine monitoring and reporting |
| Forecast deviation manageable within delegated authority | Amber | Lead PM assesses and assigns corrective actions |
| High risk (score ≥ 15) | Red | Prompt Sponsor escalation |
| Forecast breach of approved budget or material baseline | Red | Sponsor review and formal decision |
| Material legal, regulatory, privacy, or security concern | Red | Immediate escalation and appropriate incident or compliance response |
| Critical milestone threatens approved project completion | Red | Sponsor intervention and recovery or change decision |

The reporting status must reflect the actual forecast and material exposure. A low numerical risk score does not prevent escalation of a serious legal, security, or operational issue.

### 5.3 Escalation Procedure

**Step 1 — Identification**

The Task Owner identifies the issue, records the available facts, and logs the matter in Asana or the appropriate restricted issue register.

**Step 2 — Initial Assessment**

The Lead Project Manager assesses the potential impact on:

- Scope and deliverables.
- Schedule and milestones.
- Cost and resource requirements.
- Quality and acceptance criteria.
- Legal, regulatory, and information security requirements.
- Project risks and dependencies.

**Step 3 — Escalation Brief**

For matters requiring Project Sponsor intervention, the Lead Project Manager prepares a concise Escalation Brief containing:

- Issue description and known background.
- Date identified and current status.
- Root cause or current understanding of the cause.
- Affected deliverables and project baseline.
- Potential schedule, cost, and risk impact.
- Available response options, with advantages and disadvantages.
- Recommended action.
- Requested decision and required response date.

Where the issue involves an urgent security or legal incident, immediate protective action and incident reporting must not be delayed while a written brief is prepared.

**Step 4 — Decision and Documentation**

The authorized decision-maker records the decision and any required conditions.

The Lead Project Manager updates the relevant project registers, action records, and baselines where formally approved.

**Decision Timing:** For ordinary governance matters, a response deadline should be agreed according to the impact and urgency, normally within one or two business days where practicable. Critical incidents require immediate escalation and handling under the applicable response and legal procedures.

### 5.4 Change and Approval Authority

| Decision Type | Primary Authority |
|---|---|
| Routine task execution and internal coordination | Task Owner |
| Technical implementation within approved architecture and scope | Lead Software & Cloud Engineer |
| Task resequencing within approved baselines and delegated authority | Lead Project Manager |
| Risk tracking and mitigation coordination | Lead Project Manager |
| Material scope changes | Project Sponsor |
| Budget baseline changes or expenditure beyond delegated limits | Project Sponsor |
| Material schedule baseline changes | Project Sponsor |
| Legal interpretation and specialist contractual advice | External Legal Adviser |
| Approval and execution of client contracts | Authorized Company Signatory |
| Formal project closure | Project Sponsor or authorized delegate |

The External Legal Adviser provides professional legal review and recommendations. The adviser does not automatically hold authority to approve company expenditure, business commitments, or changes to project baselines.

## 6. Security, Data Privacy & Information Handling

### 6.1 Information Classification

Project information must be handled according to its confidentiality and business sensitivity.

| Classification | Examples | Handling Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Approved public website content and published service catalogue | May be distributed through approved public channels |
| Internal | General project updates, non-sensitive task records, internal procedures | Restricted to authorized project participants and approved systems |
| Confidential | Commercial proposals, client information, contractual documents, financial details | Access restricted to authorized personnel; use approved business channels |
| Restricted / Sensitive | Credentials, private keys, sensitive personal data, security incident details, privileged legal material | Secure storage and strictly controlled access; never publish in public repositories or transmit through unauthorized channels |

### 6.2 Credential and Secret Management

- Passwords, API keys, AWS credentials, SSH private keys, database credentials, and access tokens must never be committed to GitHub.
- Credentials must be maintained in approved secure secret storage or a suitable password manager.
- Email and messaging channels must not be used to transmit passwords, private keys, or access tokens.
- Access must follow least-privilege principles and be restricted to authorized personnel.
- MFA must be enabled for supported privileged and administrative accounts.
- Contractor and contributor access must be reviewed and revoked when no longer required.
- Secret detection and preventive repository controls should be enabled where supported.
- Any suspected exposure of credentials must be reported immediately and handled through the security incident procedure.

### 6.3 Personal Data Protection

Project communications and client outreach must comply with applicable Omani personal data protection requirements, including Royal Decree 6/2022 as amended by Royal Decree 68/2026 and the applicable executive regulations.

The following controls apply:

- Personal data must be collected and processed for defined and legitimate business purposes.
- Required notices, consent, permits, and other legal conditions must be assessed before processing begins.
- Personal data must be accessed only by authorized personnel and limited to business necessity.
- Personal data must not be stored in public GitHub repositories.
- Appropriate technical and organizational security controls must be implemented based on the nature and sensitivity of the information.
- Retention and deletion must follow applicable legal obligations and approved organizational procedures.
- Cloud providers and other third-party processors must be assessed before they are allowed to process relevant personal data.
- Any cross-border transfer of personal data must be assessed against applicable consent, protection, and authorization requirements.
- The organization must identify and appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) in accordance with applicable requirements.

### 6.4 Commercial Marketing and Client Outreach

Before dispatching commercial marketing communications involving personal data:

- Determine the applicable legal requirements for the intended outreach.
- Obtain and retain written, documentable consent where required by Omani law.
- Provide an appropriate means for recipients to withdraw consent or opt out.
- Stop the relevant marketing communications promptly when a valid withdrawal is received.
- Restrict access to prospect and client contact records.
- Retain only the personal information required for the approved business purpose.

Formal proposals must use approved commercial templates and must not make commitments outside the company's authorized capabilities.

### 6.5 Data Breach and Security Incident Notification

Any suspected personal data breach or significant cybersecurity incident must be reported internally immediately through the approved incident channel.

The initial response must include:

1. Reporting the incident to the Lead Project Manager and designated Security Owner.
2. Informing the designated DPO and Project Sponsor as appropriate.
3. Taking reasonable steps to contain the incident and protect affected systems and information.
4. Preserving relevant evidence and documenting the known facts.
5. Assessing the incident's scope, impact, affected data, and applicable legal notification obligations.
6. Coordinating required external communications through authorized personnel.

Under the applicable Omani regulatory framework, the controller must notify the Ministry within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach that threatens the rights of affected data subjects. The data subject must also be notified within 72 hours where the breach presents serious harm or high risk, subject to the applicable requirements.

The DPO and qualified legal adviser must assess whether the relevant reporting thresholds have been met and coordinate required notifications. Internal escalation must not delay any applicable statutory notification deadline.

Incident details must be retained in a restricted Incident Register and must not be circulated through general project communication channels.

### 6.6 Public and Media Communications

No public announcement, press release, LinkedIn publication, or external statement concerning Project Genesis, confidential project activities, or client engagements may be made without prior written approval from the Project Sponsor or an authorized representative.

Approved public communication must not disclose confidential, personal, contractual, or security-sensitive information.

## 7. Meeting Records, Reporting & Performance Monitoring

### 7.1 Weekly Status Reporting

The Lead Project Manager will prepare a weekly report containing:

- Overall project RAG status.
- Progress against deliverables D1–D7.
- Milestone progress and forecast completion.
- Budget position against the approved cost baseline.
- Significant risks and issues.
- Outstanding decisions and approvals.
- Actions due and responsible owners.
- Priorities for the next reporting period.

The report must distinguish between the initial planning budget estimate and the formally approved cost baseline.

### 7.2 Communication Effectiveness

The Lead Project Manager will review communication effectiveness throughout the project.

Relevant indicators may include:

- Timeliness of required project updates.
- Percentage of material decisions documented.
- Number of overdue communication-related action items.
- Time taken to escalate significant blockers.
- Completion of required approvals before dependent work proceeds.
- Availability of supporting records for milestone acceptance.

Communication effectiveness is assessed by the quality, timeliness, accuracy, and traceability of information rather than the volume of meetings or messages.

### 7.3 Continuous Improvement

Communication practices may be adjusted based on stakeholder feedback, project complexity, resource availability, and actual delivery needs.

Material changes to the communication approach must be reviewed by the Lead Project Manager and documented when they affect governance responsibilities or approved communication requirements.
