## 1. Executive Summary and Communication Objectives

### 1.1 Purpose

This Project Communication Management Plan defines the communication framework, stakeholder information requirements, communication channels, reporting arrangements, meeting practices, documentation standards, and escalation procedures necessary to support Project Genesis.

Project Genesis is a real business and operational readiness initiative undertaken within Bridge of Tomorrow, an Oman-registered company with an active Commercial Registration (CR).

The plan covers communication across:

- Project governance and authorization.
- Project planning and monitoring.
- Technical implementation and cloud infrastructure.
- Operational workflow development.
- Commercial and legal review.
- Data protection and information security.
- Service catalogue development.
- Client outreach and business development.
- Deliverable acceptance and project closure.

### 1.2 Communication Objectives

The communication management objectives are to:

- Maintain transparency regarding project progress, deliverables, schedule, budget, and material dependencies.
- Establish clear communication between the Project Manager, Project Sponsor, authorized company representatives, and assigned project contributors.
- Provide concise and decision-oriented management reporting.
- Support coordination through Asana, GitHub, Google Workspace, and other approved channels.
- Maintain traceable records of decisions, approvals, issues, change requests, and action items.
- Ensure material risks, blockers, and deviations are escalated appropriately.
- Protect confidential business information, personal data, and security-sensitive information.
- Support communication with external advisers, service providers, regulatory authorities, and SME prospects.
- Distinguish informal coordination and verbal endorsement from formally recorded approvals.
- Maintain consistent communication responsibilities throughout the project lifecycle.

### 1.3 Communication Principles

**1. Audience-Appropriate Communication**

Information must be tailored to the recipient's responsibilities, decision-making authority, and information requirements.

Management communications should focus on project progress, material risks, deviations, business impact, and required decisions.

Technical communications should provide sufficient detail to support implementation, troubleshooting, security review, and verification.

Commercial and legal communications must use approved business information and appropriately restricted channels.

**2. TL;DR Reporting Principle**

Executive status reports, significant escalation messages, and decision requests should begin with a concise summary identifying:

- Overall situation or project status.
- Key issue, risk, or decision required.
- Relevant deadline or requested action.

Supporting details and evidence should follow where necessary.

**3. RAG Status Reporting**

Red, Amber, and Green indicators will be used consistently in project status reports and milestone reviews.

| Status | Definition | Management Response |
|---|---|---|
| Green — On Track | Work is progressing according to the applicable approved baseline or latest reviewed planning assumptions, with no unresolved critical concern. | Continue monitoring and routine reporting. |
| Amber — At Risk | A potential deviation, dependency, resource constraint, or material issue exists but can reasonably be managed within delegated authority. | Assign corrective actions, monitor closely, and report to the Project Manager. |
| Red — Critical | An approved baseline is forecast to be breached, a High risk or serious incident requires intervention, or a decision beyond delegated authority is necessary. | Escalate promptly to the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative. |

Until the relevant project baselines are formally approved, RAG assessments must not imply that an approved schedule or cost baseline exists.

The Project Manager must distinguish performance against planning assumptions from performance against authorized baselines.

**4. 5W1H Communication Alignment**

Each planned communication event should identify:

- **Who:** Intended audience and responsible owner.
- **What:** Information, update, or decision being communicated.
- **When:** Frequency, timing, or triggering event.
- **Where:** Approved communication channel.
- **Why:** Purpose and expected outcome.
- **How:** Format, supporting evidence, and record location.

**5. Transparency and Traceability**

Material decisions, changes, approvals, commitments, and exceptions must be documented in the appropriate project record.

Informal messages and verbal discussions do not replace required formal approvals.

The Project Sponsor's verbal endorsement of Project Genesis must be distinguished from formal approval of the Charter, communication plan, or project baselines.

**6. Information Security**

Confidential information, personal data, credentials, and security-sensitive material must be handled through approved channels and according to organizational access and information-handling requirements.

Public repository content must be limited to material approved for public disclosure.

### 3.2 Channel Usage Guidelines

**1. GitHub Repository**

GitHub serves as the version-controlled repository for:

- Project management documents approved for public disclosure.
- Non-sensitive technical documentation.
- Sanitized architecture descriptions and implementation records.
- Approved SOPs and technical artifacts.
- Relevant non-confidential project records.

Public repositories must not contain confidential company information, personal data, private legal documents, sensitive CR information, credentials, or restricted financial records.

Signed legal originals and confidential commercial records must remain in appropriately controlled company storage unless a restricted repository has been explicitly approved.

**2. Asana Workspace**

The organization-controlled Asana workspace supports:

- Task management and assignment.
- WBS work-package tracking.
- Dependencies and blockers.
- Milestone progress.
- Action-item tracking.
- Operational checklists and workflows.
- Project status coordination.

Relevant tasks should reference the corresponding WBS code and identify Responsible and Accountable roles.

**3. Google Workspace**

Google Workspace is used for:

- Official corporate emails.
- Management reporting.
- Legal template reviews.
- Commercial proposal preparation and dispatch.
- Controlled business documents.
- Formal approvals and supporting records.

Access to restricted documents must be limited to authorized individuals.

**4. Google Meet**

Google Meet may be used for:

- Stakeholder meetings.
- Project status discussions.
- Technical and operational reviews.
- Deliverable demonstrations.
- Lessons learned sessions.
- Legal and commercial review meetings where appropriate.

Invitations should include purpose, participants, agenda, and relevant reference materials.

**5. Approved Secure Messaging**

Approved messaging channels may support:

- Time-sensitive operational coordination.
- Short progress updates.
- Immediate blocker notification.
- Meeting coordination.
- Urgent communication requiring prompt attention.

Messaging is not the authoritative record for formal approvals, contractual commitments, or sensitive information.

Material decisions communicated through messaging must be documented in the relevant project record, normally within one business day.

Passwords, private keys, API keys, database credentials, and access tokens must never be transmitted through general messaging channels.

## 4. Meeting Management and Documentation

### 4.1 Meeting Preparation

Meetings should be organized according to purpose and involve only stakeholders required to contribute, review, or decide.

Where appropriate:

- Agendas should be shared before scheduled meetings.
- Participants should receive relevant documents in advance.
- Required decisions and expected outcomes should be identified.
- Meeting duration should be proportionate to the subject.
- Confidential materials should be distributed through restricted channels.
- Meetings requiring formal decisions must identify the authorized decision-maker.
- Daily coordination should remain asynchronous by default.

### 4.2 Meeting Types

| Meeting Type | Purpose | Participants | Proposed Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily Coordination | Review tasks, dependencies, blockers, and priorities | Project Manager and assigned contributors | Weekdays, asynchronous by default |
| Weekly Project Review | Review progress, RAG status, risks, issues, and decisions | Project Manager and Sponsor or authorized representative when designated | Weekly |
| Technical Review | Review configuration, architecture, security, and implementation | Project Manager and assigned technical contributor | Active technical delivery and major checkpoints |
| Legal and Commercial Review | Review templates, service descriptions, and legal requirements | Project Manager, Legal Adviser when engaged, authorized representative as required | Relevant review milestones |
| Deliverable Review | Demonstrate and assess deliverable readiness | Project Manager, assigned contributors, and authorized approver | Relevant milestone or iteration completion |
| Project Closure Review | Review handover, open items, lessons learned, and final acceptance | Project Manager, Sponsor or authorized representative, and asset owners | During closure |

### 4.3 Meeting Minutes and Action Tracking

Meeting records should contain:

- Meeting title and date.
- Participants and roles.
- Key discussion points.
- Decisions and approval outcomes.
- Issues and dependencies.
- Assigned action items.
- Action owners and target dates.
- Relevant WBS and document references.

Material meeting notes and action records should normally be published within one business day.

Confidential or personal information must be stored in restricted locations.

### 4.4 Decision Documentation

Material decisions must be recorded in the Project Decision Log or appropriate approval record.

Each decision should include:

- Decision reference.
- Date and decision authority.
- Description and rationale.
- Affected scope, schedule, budget, or deliverable.
- Alternatives considered where relevant.
- Required follow-up action.
- Approval reference.

Informal discussions must not be treated as formal authorization until the required approval is recorded.

### 4.5 Action Item Management

Actions must be:

- Clearly described.
- Assigned to one accountable owner.
- Given an agreed target date.
- Recorded in Asana or the appropriate controlled register.
- Reviewed during subsequent coordination.
- Escalated when overdue actions threaten delivery or compliance.

## 5. Escalation Framework and Decision Authority

### 5.1 Escalation Levels

**Level 1 — Operational / Assigned Task Owner**

Responsible roles include assigned contributors and other formally engaged resources.

Decision authority includes:

- Day-to-day execution of assigned tasks.
- Routine adjustments within approved requirements.
- Resolution of minor blockers within work-package boundaries.
- Internal coordination and progress reporting.

Escalation triggers include:

- A task blocker remains unresolved for more than one business day.
- A dependency prevents expected progress.
- A task is forecast to miss its agreed target.
- A security, privacy, or regulatory concern is identified.

The Task Owner must document the issue and notify the Project Manager.

**Level 2 — Project Management / Project Manager**

Responsible authority: Project Manager — Masoud Shafiezadeh Neyestanak.

Decision authority includes:

- Task resequencing within approved scope and delegated authority.
- Coordination of available resources.
- Monitoring mitigation actions.
- Vendor follow-up.
- Review of schedule and cost forecasts.
- Recommendation of corrective action and change requests.
- Coordination of issue resolution and stakeholder communication.

Escalation triggers to Level 3 include:

- A forecast schedule variance exceeds two business days or threatens an approved milestone.
- Forecast expenditure exceeds the approved budget or delegated authority.
- A material scope change is required.
- A risk receives a High rating (score of 15 or higher).
- A significant legal, regulatory, privacy, or security concern is identified.
- A decision exceeds the Project Manager's delegated authority.
- A material resource constraint threatens an agreed deliverable.

Until schedule or cost baselines are formally approved, concerns must be assessed against reviewed planning assumptions and existing company spending authority.

**Level 3 — Project Governance / Project Sponsor or Authorized Company Representative**

Responsible authority: Project Sponsor or formally authorized company representative, following confirmation of applicable decision authority.

Decision authority, subject to formal delegation, includes:

- Approval of material project scope changes.
- Approval of changes to an authorized Cost Baseline.
- Approval of material Schedule Baseline revisions.
- Resolution of escalated stakeholder conflicts.
- Decisions concerning material residual risks.
- Decisions concerning project suspension or termination.
- Authorization of material commercial commitments within delegated company authority.
- Approval of final project closure.

Escalation triggers include:

- A forecast schedule delay exceeds five business days.
- An approved budget baseline is forecast to be exceeded.
- A High risk requires formal acceptance or additional resources.
- A material legal, regulatory, privacy, or cybersecurity concern arises.
- A formal baseline change requires Sponsor approval.
- The Project Manager requires a decision beyond delegated authority.

Serious security incidents, suspected personal data breaches, and material regulatory concerns must be escalated immediately, irrespective of routine schedule or financial thresholds.

### 5.3 Escalation Procedure

**Step 1 — Identification**

The Task Owner or relevant stakeholder identifies and records the issue through Asana or the appropriate restricted issue register.

Security and privacy incidents must follow the approved incident reporting process immediately.

**Step 2 — Initial Assessment**

The Project Manager assesses impact on scope, schedule, cost, quality, security, regulatory requirements, resources, and business operations.

**Step 3 — Escalation Brief**

For matters requiring governance intervention, the Project Manager prepares a concise brief containing:

- Issue description and background.
- Date identified and current status.
- Known or suspected cause.
- Affected deliverables and baselines or planning assumptions.
- Potential schedule, cost, and risk impact.
- Response options.
- Recommended action.
- Requested decision and response date.

Urgent protective action and statutory incident reporting must not be delayed while a written brief is prepared.

**Step 4 — Decision and Documentation**

The authorized decision-maker records the decision and applicable conditions.

The Project Manager updates the relevant records and baselines where authorized.

Ordinary governance decisions should normally be responded to within an agreed one- or two-business-day period where practicable.

Critical incidents must follow immediate escalation and applicable statutory procedures.

### 5.4 Approval and Decision Traceability

- The Project Manager may coordinate ordinary operational decisions within delegated authority.
- Material scope, schedule, and budget changes require formal approval.
- Commercial commitments must be authorized by the appropriate company signatory.
- Legal advisers provide professional advice but do not automatically hold company approval authority.
- Verbal Sponsor endorsement does not constitute formal baseline approval.
- Material approvals must be retained in controlled company records.

## 6. Security, Data Privacy, and Information Handling

### 6.1 Information Classification

| Classification | Examples | Handling Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Approved website content, published service catalogue, sanitized project documentation | May be distributed through approved public channels |
| Internal | General project updates, non-sensitive task records, internal procedures | Restricted to authorized project participants |
| Confidential | Commercial proposals, client information, contracts, financial details, non-public business information | Access restricted to authorized personnel |
| Restricted / Sensitive | Credentials, private keys, sensitive personal data, incident details, privileged legal material | Secure storage and strictly controlled access; never publish in public repositories |

Where a document contains information belonging to multiple classifications, the more restrictive handling requirement applies.

### 6.2 Credential and Secret Management

- Passwords, API keys, AWS credentials, SSH private keys, database credentials, and access tokens must never be committed to GitHub.
- Credentials must be maintained in approved secure storage or an appropriate password manager.
- Email and messaging channels must not transmit passwords, private keys, or access tokens.
- Access must follow least-privilege principles.
- MFA must be enabled for supported privileged accounts.
- Contractor and contributor access must be reviewed and revoked when no longer required.
- Secret detection and preventive repository controls should be enabled where supported.
- Suspected credential exposure must be reported immediately.
- Public project documents must be checked for accidental disclosure before publication.

### 6.3 Personal Data Protection

Project communications and client outreach must comply with applicable Omani personal data protection requirements, including:

- Royal Decree 6/2022 — Personal Data Protection Law.
- Royal Decree 68/2026 — amendments to certain provisions of the Personal Data Protection Law.
- Ministerial Decision 34/2024 — Executive Regulation of the Personal Data Protection Law, to the extent applicable following subsequent amendments.

The following controls apply:

- Personal data must be collected and processed for defined and legitimate business purposes.
- Applicable legal conditions for processing must be identified before processing begins.
- Consent must be obtained and documented where required by law.
- Commercial marketing communications must follow applicable consent and withdrawal requirements.
- Personal data must be accessed only by authorized personnel and limited to business necessity.
- Personal data must not be stored in public GitHub repositories.
- Appropriate technical and organizational security controls must be implemented.
- Retention and deletion must follow applicable legal requirements and approved procedures.
- Cloud providers and third-party processors must be assessed before processing relevant personal data.
- Cross-border transfers must be assessed against applicable legal and authorization requirements.
- Bridge of Tomorrow's obligations as a controller or processor must be assessed against its actual activities.
- Where applicable requirements make DPO designation necessary, the company must arrange the required formal designation.
- The DPO's identity and responsibilities must be recorded in controlled company documentation.

The DPO and qualified legal adviser must be involved where their responsibilities apply.

DPO designation does not transfer the controller's legal accountability to the DPO.

### 6.4 Commercial Marketing and Client Outreach

Before dispatching marketing communications involving personal data:

- Determine applicable legal requirements.
- Identify the appropriate legal basis for processing and communication.
- Obtain and retain consent where required.
- Record relevant consent evidence and its source.
- Provide an appropriate withdrawal or opt-out mechanism where applicable.
- Stop relevant communications promptly after a valid withdrawal.
- Restrict access to prospect and client contact records.
- Retain only personal information required for the approved purpose.
- Use authorized commercial descriptions and terms.
- Assess whether using contact information from an external source is permitted.

### 6.5 Data Breach and Security Incident Notification

Any suspected personal data breach or significant cybersecurity incident must be reported internally immediately through the approved incident channel.

The initial response must include:

1. Reporting the incident to the designated Security Owner, once appointed, and the Project Manager.
2. Informing the DPO where appointed and authorized company representative as appropriate.
3. Taking reasonable steps to contain the incident.
4. Preserving relevant evidence and documenting known facts.
5. Assessing scope, impact, affected data, and applicable notification obligations.
6. Coordinating required external communications through authorized personnel.

Under the applicable Omani Personal Data Protection framework, the controller may have a 72-hour notification obligation to the competent department for a qualifying personal data breach that threatens the rights of affected data subjects. Separate notification duties to affected data subjects may also apply where the statutory harm or risk threshold is met.

The DPO and qualified legal adviser must assess applicable thresholds and coordinate required notifications.

Internal escalation must not delay a statutory notification deadline.

Incident details must be maintained in a restricted Incident Register.

The applicable legal requirements must be verified against the current law and regulations at the time of an incident.

### 6.6 Public and External Communications

No public announcement, press release, LinkedIn publication, or external statement concerning confidential Project Genesis activities, internal decisions, or client engagements may be made without the required company approval.

Approved communications must not disclose confidential company information, personal or client data, private contracts, sensitive CR details, credentials, security-sensitive information, or unverified service claims.

Public documentation of Project Genesis must remain consistent with company confidentiality and information security requirements.
