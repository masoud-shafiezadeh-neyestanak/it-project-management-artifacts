## 1. Purpose and Schedule Management Objectives

### 1.1 Purpose

This document establishes the proposed schedule framework for Project Genesis, a real business and operational readiness initiative undertaken within Bridge of Tomorrow.

It translates the 28 lowest-level WBS work packages into an activity-based delivery plan, identifying preliminary durations, sequencing, dependencies, milestones, and responsibility assignments.

The schedule provides a structured reference for coordinating activities, monitoring progress, managing dependencies, identifying delays, and supporting timely decisions.

It will become the authorized Schedule Baseline only after activity estimates, resource assignments, working calendar, dependencies, and approval requirements have been reviewed and formally approved.

### 1.2 Objectives

The schedule management objectives are to:

- Translate all 28 lowest-level WBS work packages into a coordinated activity schedule.
- Establish preliminary duration estimates and delivery windows.
- Identify logical activity dependencies and sequencing requirements.
- Define common project milestones and completion evidence.
- Align schedule ownership with GEN-STK-001.
- Coordinate technical, commercial, legal, and operational workstreams.
- Identify activities that may be performed concurrently.
- Monitor progress and forecast milestone completion.
- Identify critical dependencies and potential delays.
- Provide a basis for corrective action and formal schedule change control.

### 1.3 Schedule Baseline Components

The proposed Schedule Baseline contains:

1. Activity identification and decomposition based on the Scope Baseline.
2. Activity duration estimates.
3. Logical sequencing and dependency relationships.
4. Planned activity windows and milestones.
5. Responsibility and resource considerations.
6. Schedule monitoring and change-control arrangements.
7. Formal approval requirements.

The Schedule Baseline is separate from the Scope Baseline and Cost Baseline, but must remain consistent with both.

## 2. Schedule Planning Assumptions and Methodology

### 2.1 Project Duration

The planned project duration is eight calendar weeks, nominally equivalent to 40 business days under a five-day working-week assumption.

This is a planning target, not an approved calendar commitment.

Actual start and target completion dates must be confirmed after project authorization and review of resource availability.

### 2.2 Working Calendar

The initial schedule assumes:

- Five working days per week.
- Activity durations expressed in working days.
- Planned delivery windows identified as Week 1 through Week 8.
- Project date and time records maintained in Oman Standard Time (GST, UTC+4).

The company's actual working days, weekend arrangement, public holidays, and non-working days must be confirmed before calendar dates are assigned.

### 2.3 Duration Estimation Method

Activity durations are preliminary estimates prepared by the Project Manager using:

- The current WBS decomposition.
- Expected work-package complexity.
- Known technical and operational dependencies.
- Proposed delivery sequence.
- Current resource assumptions.

These estimates are planning judgments. They are not validated commitments from contractors, external advisers, or formally assigned contributors.

Where specialist resources are engaged, their estimates and availability must be reviewed before the relevant activity durations are approved.

### 2.4 Duration and Effort Distinction

Activity duration and work effort are different measures.

- **Activity Duration:** Planned working time required to complete a work package.
- **Work Effort:** Labor required, normally expressed in person-hours or person-days.
- **Elapsed Time:** Total time between activity start and finish, including approved waiting periods and external dependencies where applicable.

This schedule primarily estimates activity duration. Detailed effort estimates and resource loading remain subject to confirmation.

A work package may have a delivery window spanning multiple weeks while requiring only a limited number of actual working days. Its planned window identifies when the work can occur; it does not mean that the resource is continuously occupied for the entire period.

### 2.5 Scheduling Logic

The following rules apply:

- WBS codes maintain traceability between scope and schedule activities.
- Finish-to-Start (FS) is the default dependency relationship unless otherwise specified.
- Activities may be performed concurrently when their prerequisites, resource availability, and approval requirements allow.
- An activity must not begin before its essential inputs and required authorizations are available.
- Website testing must be completed before production release.
- Commercial proposals must not be dispatched before relevant service information and commercial terms are authorized.
- Acceptance and handover activities may be performed progressively as deliverables become available.
- Final project closure remains dependent on the required acceptance, handover, and lessons learned activities.

Where actual resource availability cannot support proposed concurrent activities, the schedule must be revised.

### 4.3 Closeout Activity Sequencing

Acceptance review and asset handover are planned as progressive activities during Weeks 7–8.

This means:

- D1–D5 may be reviewed as their completion evidence becomes available.
- Asset inventories and handover documentation may be prepared before final project closeout.
- Final acceptance of D6 follows the dispatch of the five proposals.
- Final asset handover confirmation is completed before closeout approval.
- Lessons learned are finalized after the main delivery activities and relevant review findings are available.
- The Closeout Report is finalized after acceptance review, handover, and lessons learned are complete.

The two-day estimates for acceptance and asset handover represent estimated work duration, not an assumption that all preparation begins and ends sequentially within Week 8.

The Project Manager must establish day-level activities in Asana to verify that the final closeout sequence is feasible within the planned working calendar.

## 6. Schedule Monitoring and Control

### 6.1 Progress Monitoring

The Project Manager will monitor activity progress through the designated Asana workspace and controlled project records.

Progress monitoring includes:

- Planned start and finish dates once confirmed.
- Actual start and finish dates.
- Estimated remaining duration.
- Activity completion percentage where supported by measurable work.
- Predecessor and successor status.
- Current blockers and dependencies.
- Resource availability.
- Forecast milestone completion.
- Schedule variance against the applicable reference schedule.

Activities must not be marked complete solely because their planned duration has elapsed.

### 6.2 Schedule Status Reporting

| Status | Condition | Management Response |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Progress is consistent with the approved schedule or latest reviewed planning assumptions, with no unresolved material dependency. | Continue monitoring and routine reporting. |
| Amber | A forecast delay, resource constraint, unresolved dependency, or approval delay exists but may be managed within delegated authority. | Assess impact and assign corrective actions. |
| Red | An approved milestone or schedule baseline is forecast to be breached, or a material decision beyond delegated authority is required. | Escalate to the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative. |

Before schedule approval, RAG assessments must be described as performance against the proposed planning schedule rather than an approved Schedule Baseline.

### 6.3 Schedule Variance

Once the Schedule Baseline is formally approved, the Project Manager will monitor the difference between planned and actual or forecast activity dates.

**Schedule Variance (Days) = Forecast Finish Date − Baseline Finish Date**

The same agreed working calendar must be used when calculating the variance.

A positive value indicates a forecast delay relative to the baseline.

### 6.4 Milestone Forecasting

Milestone forecasts will be reviewed at least weekly.

Forecasting must consider:

- Current activity progress.
- Remaining duration.
- Unresolved dependencies.
- Stakeholder review time.
- Resource availability.
- Supplier and contractor lead times.
- Regulatory and legal review requirements.
- Approved changes and constraints.

### 6.5 Escalation Thresholds

| Condition | Escalation Level | Required Action |
|---|---|---|
| Minor task delay with no milestone impact | Level 1 | Assigned owner coordinates corrective action and informs the Project Manager. |
| Forecast delay exceeding 2 business days or threatening an approved milestone | Level 2 | Project Manager assesses impact, assigns corrective action, and reports the exposure. |
| Forecast delay exceeding 5 business days or materially threatening project completion | Level 3 | Escalate to the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative. |
| Delay caused by legal, regulatory, privacy, or security concerns | Immediate escalation according to severity | Protect the organization and comply with applicable requirements. |

Before baseline approval, delays must be measured against the latest reviewed planning schedule and identified as forecast variances.

### 6.6 Corrective and Recovery Actions

Where schedule deviations occur, the Project Manager may recommend:

- Resequencing activities where dependencies allow.
- Prioritizing work packages affecting major milestones.
- Removing unnecessary task dependencies.
- Requesting additional qualified resources, subject to authorization.
- Adjusting delivery sequencing.
- Reviewing supplier or contractor arrangements.
- Reducing non-essential scope through formal change control.
- Revising the target completion date where justified.

Schedule compression must not compromise legal review, security verification, quality, or required approvals.

## 7. Critical Path and Dependency Analysis

### 7.1 Critical Path Status

A formally validated Critical Path has not yet been established.

A complete Critical Path Method analysis requires:

- Confirmed activity durations.
- Validated dependency relationships.
- Confirmed working calendar.
- Actual or committed resource availability.
- Review of concurrent activities and resource conflicts.
- Confirmed project start date.
- Assessment of total and free float.

Until these inputs are validated, no activity sequence is represented as the approved Critical Path.

### 7.2 Preliminary Critical Dependencies

**Digital Infrastructure to Website Release**

Domain and DNS provisioning (1.2.1), corporate workspace and email setup (1.2.2), and identity and access controls (1.2.3) support website implementation, testing, and production release.

Website Quality & Security Testing (1.3.4) must be completed before Production Website Release (1.3.3).

**Commercial Templates to Proposal Dispatch**

Preparation of SOW/NDA templates (1.4.1), MSA/SLA templates (1.4.2), and the Commercial Proposal Template (1.4.3) precedes the required legal review (1.4.4).

Proposal customization (1.7.2) depends on prospect qualification, the reviewed templates, and authorized commercial terms.

**Service Verification to Catalogue and Outreach**

CR Activity Verification & Service Mapping (1.6.1) precedes preparation and publication of the service catalogue (1.6.2 and 1.6.3).

Verified service information also supports prospect qualification and proposal customization.

**Deliverables to Project Closure**

Deliverable Acceptance Review (1.8.1), Asset Handover (1.8.2), and Lessons Learned (1.8.3) are required inputs to the Closeout Report and Sponsor Sign-Off (1.8.4).

These sequences are preliminary dependency relationships and are not a substitute for a validated CPM analysis.

### 7.3 Float and Schedule Reserve

Activity float has not been formally calculated.

No schedule reserve has been approved.

External legal review, resource assignment, supplier provisioning, content approval, and Sponsor decisions may affect schedule feasibility and must be reviewed during schedule validation.

## 8. Resource Coordination and Schedule Constraints

The schedule uses functional roles aligned with GEN-STK-001.

No specialist activity should be treated as fully resourced merely because its role appears in the schedule.

Before an activity is committed:

- The relevant resource must be assigned or engaged.
- Its availability must be confirmed.
- Required inputs and dependencies must be identified.
- Any additional expenditure must be authorized.
- Specialist work must be performed or verified by a suitably qualified resource.

Where a resource gap threatens delivery, the Project Manager must document the impact and submit appropriate options to the authorized company representative.

The Project Manager retains coordination responsibility but does not replace specialist technical, legal, or regulatory expertise.

## 9. Schedule Change Control

Any material change affecting the approved schedule must be documented before implementation.

A Schedule Change Request should include:

- Description and reason for the change.
- Relevant WBS activity or milestone.
- Current baseline and forecast dates, where applicable.
- Impact on predecessor and successor activities.
- Impact on the overall completion target.
- Resource and cost implications.
- Relevant project risks.
- Alternatives and recommended action.
- Required approval authority.

The Project Manager will assess the request and provide a recommendation.

Changes to an approved Schedule Baseline require authorization by the Project Sponsor or formally authorized delegate.

Approved changes must be reflected in:

- Schedule Baseline.
- Asana tasks and milestones.
- Project Status Reports.
- Decision Log and Change Log where applicable.
- Related budget and risk records where affected.
