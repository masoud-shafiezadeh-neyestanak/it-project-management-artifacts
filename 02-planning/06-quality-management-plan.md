## 1. Purpose and Quality Management Objectives

### 1.1 Purpose

This Quality Management Plan defines the quality objectives, standards, methods, responsibilities, verification activities, acceptance arrangements, and quality-control procedures applicable to Project Genesis.

Project Genesis is a real business and operational readiness initiative undertaken within Bridge of Tomorrow, an Oman-registered company with an active Commercial Registration (CR).

The project involves establishing and strengthening the company's digital infrastructure, corporate web presence, commercial and legal documentation, operational workflows, digital service catalogue, and initial client outreach capabilities.

The purpose of this plan is to ensure that project deliverables are developed, reviewed, tested, documented, and accepted according to defined requirements and appropriate evidence.

Quality management applies throughout the project lifecycle, from planning and requirements definition to implementation, verification, acceptance, asset handover, and project closure.

### 1.2 Quality Management Objectives

The quality management objectives are to:

- Ensure that all seven project deliverables satisfy their documented scope and acceptance criteria.
- Establish clear quality requirements for technical, commercial, legal, and operational outputs.
- Prevent avoidable defects through appropriate planning, review, and verification.
- Detect and document nonconformities before deliverables are accepted or released.
- Ensure that technical configurations are tested against agreed requirements.
- Ensure that corporate information and service descriptions are accurate and authorized.
- Ensure that commercial and legal templates receive the required professional and business reviews.
- Maintain appropriate security and access-control practices.
- Preserve evidence of quality reviews, tests, approvals, and corrective actions.
- Ensure that deliverable acceptance is supported by evidence and authorized decisions.
- Apply lessons learned to improve subsequent project activities and future business operations.

### 1.3 Quality Management Principles

The following principles apply throughout Project Genesis.

**1. Quality Is Planned**

Quality requirements and acceptance criteria must be identified before the relevant work is accepted.

**2. Prevention Before Correction**

Requirements reviews, technical design reviews, document reviews, and security controls should reduce the likelihood of defects before they occur.

**3. Verification Through Evidence**

A task being marked complete in Asana, a website being published, or a document being uploaded to GitHub does not automatically demonstrate that it meets the required quality criteria.

**4. Accountability and Appropriate Expertise**

The Project Manager coordinates quality management and acceptance activities. Specialist technical testing, professional legal review, and other specialist verification must be performed or supported by appropriately qualified resources.

**5. Controlled Approval**

Only an appropriately authorized company representative may provide formal business acceptance or approve commitments within the person's delegated authority.

**6. Continuous Improvement**

Quality findings, feedback, and lessons learned must be documented and used to improve project delivery.

**7. Proportionate Quality Controls**

Quality checks must be appropriate to the complexity, risk, purpose, and business importance of each deliverable.

## 2. Quality Management Approach

### 2.1 Quality Assurance and Quality Control

Project Genesis distinguishes between Quality Assurance (QA) and Quality Control (QC).

| Area | Quality Assurance (QA) | Quality Control (QC) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Focus | Preventing quality problems through suitable processes and working practices | Detecting defects or nonconformities in completed or developing outputs |
| Nature | Process-oriented | Deliverable-oriented |
| Timing | Throughout planning and execution | During reviews, inspections, testing, and acceptance |
| Examples | Reviewing requirements, defining standards, checking document control, confirming responsibility assignments | Website functional testing, DNS verification, document review, access testing, and acceptance checks |
| Main Output | Improved processes and reduced likelihood of defects | Test results, review findings, defect records, and acceptance evidence |

Both QA and QC are required.

A satisfactory process does not guarantee that every deliverable is defect-free, and a successful individual test does not demonstrate that the overall delivery process is adequately controlled.

### 2.2 Quality Management Activities

Quality management will include:

- Requirements and acceptance-criteria reviews.
- Document and configuration reviews.
- Technical inspections and functional testing.
- Content and business-information verification.
- Legal and commercial review where required.
- Security and access-control verification.
- Review of project records and deliverable evidence.
- Defect identification and corrective action.
- Formal deliverable acceptance.
- Quality lessons learned and improvement actions.

### 2.3 Quality Standards and Reference Principles

Project Genesis will use project-specific quality requirements and suitable industry practices according to the nature of each deliverable.

The project does not currently claim formal certification or compliance with an external Quality Management System standard.

Where appropriate, quality reviews may consider:

- Requirements completeness and traceability.
- Document accuracy and version control.
- Functional correctness and usability.
- Security and access management.
- Technical reliability and recoverability.
- Professional legal and commercial review.
- Consistency between approved project documents.
- Evidence-based acceptance and controlled change management.

Any decision to adopt a formal external standard or certification requirement must be assessed and approved separately.

### 2.4 Quality Control Boundaries

Quality management does not independently establish:

- Legal compliance or legal certification.
- Authorization to perform activities not covered by applicable company registration or licensing.
- Guaranteed website availability or commercial results.
- Guaranteed customer acquisition or revenue.
- Security certification or penetration-testing assurance.
- A formal external audit or certification of the company's management system.

Specialist assessments or external audits, where required, must have an approved scope, qualified provider, and authorized budget.

## 6. Quality Control and Testing Procedures

### 6.1 Quality Control Process

The following process applies to deliverables and significant work products:

1. Confirm the relevant requirement and acceptance criteria.
2. Identify the appropriate verification method.
3. Assign an appropriately qualified reviewer or tester.
4. Perform the inspection, review, or test.
5. Record the result and supporting evidence.
6. Identify and classify defects or nonconformities.
7. Assign corrective actions where necessary.
8. Retest or re-review corrected outputs.
9. Confirm whether acceptance criteria have been met.
10. Submit the deliverable for formal acceptance by the designated authorized approver.

### 6.2 Verification Methods

| Method | Description | Typical Application |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection | Examination of a document, configuration, or physical or digital output | Templates, SOPs, access configurations, project documentation |
| Functional Testing | Verification that a system or feature performs its intended function | Website, email, domain, contact forms, workflows |
| Configuration Review | Examination of technical settings and administrative controls | Cloud infrastructure, DNS, IAM, repositories |
| Content Review | Verification of accuracy, completeness, terminology, and presentation | Website, service catalogue, proposals |
| Professional Review | Specialist review by a qualified adviser | Legal templates, privacy-related requirements |
| Demonstration / Walkthrough | Practical demonstration of an output or workflow | Asana workspace, operational processes |
| Evidence Review | Examination of records showing completion or implementation | Proposal dispatch, asset handover, approvals |
| Acceptance Review | Formal assessment against deliverable acceptance criteria | D1–D7 |

### 6.3 Test Planning

For testing activities requiring multiple checks, the reviewer should prepare a test record identifying:

- Test or inspection reference.
- Related WBS and deliverable.
- Requirement being verified.
- Test objective.
- Preconditions and test environment.
- Test procedure.
- Expected result.
- Actual result.
- Pass or Fail status.
- Defect reference, if applicable.
- Reviewer and review date.
- Supporting evidence location.

Testing must be performed in an appropriate environment and must avoid unnecessary exposure of production data or sensitive information.

### 6.4 Test Evidence

Quality evidence must be sufficient to demonstrate that the relevant requirement has been verified.

Examples include:

- DNS lookup results.
- Email send and receive test results.
- MFA and access configuration checklists.
- Website functional test results.
- HTTPS verification evidence.
- Browser and responsive-layout review.
- Legal review comments.
- Asana workspace inspection.
- GitHub access and secret-control review.
- Service mapping and business approvals.
- Proposal dispatch evidence.
- Asset handover confirmation.

Screenshots may be used where helpful, provided they do not expose credentials, personal data, confidential information, or other restricted material.

## 7. Defect and Nonconformity Management

### 7.1 Definitions

**Defect:** A specific flaw or failure in a work product or deliverable that prevents it from meeting an agreed requirement.

**Nonconformity:** A failure to satisfy a documented requirement, process, specification, or approved procedure.

**Corrective Action:** An action taken to remove the cause of an identified nonconformity and reduce the likelihood of recurrence.

**Rework:** Work performed to correct a product or deliverable that does not meet the required criteria.

### 7.2 Defect Severity Classification

| Severity | Definition | Examples | Required Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | A failure that creates a serious security, legal, privacy, data-integrity, or operational risk, or prevents safe and authorized release. | Exposed credentials, serious unauthorized access, critical data loss, materially unauthorized commercial claims. | Immediate escalation; affected release or acceptance must be blocked until appropriately resolved or formally governed. |
| Major | A failure that materially affects required functionality, business accuracy, contractual integrity, or acceptance criteria. | Nonfunctional contact mechanism, incorrect material service description, missing required contractual provisions, failed access controls. | Correct before acceptance or obtain an explicitly authorized exception where appropriate. |
| Minor | A limited issue that does not materially prevent intended use or violate a critical requirement. | Formatting inconsistency, minor wording error, nonessential documentation gap. | Record and correct within an agreed period; acceptance with a documented minor issue may be considered by the authorized approver. |

Severity must reflect actual business and risk impact.

A defect must not be classified as Minor simply because the cost or effort to correct it is high.

### 7.3 Defect and Nonconformity Log

The Project Manager will maintain a Defect and Nonconformity Log.

The log should include:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Defect ID | Unique reference |
| Date Identified | Date the issue was discovered |
| WBS / Deliverable | Related work package and deliverable ID |
| Description | Clear description of the defect or nonconformity |
| Severity | Critical, Major, or Minor |
| Reported By | Person identifying the issue |
| Responsible Owner | Person responsible for corrective action |
| Root Cause | Known or suspected reason for occurrence |
| Corrective Action | Required correction or response |
| Target Date | Agreed completion date |
| Status | Open, In Progress, Blocked, Ready for Retest, or Closed |
| Verification Evidence | Test or review evidence demonstrating correction |
| Closure Approval | Authorized reviewer or approver where required |

### 7.4 Defect Resolution Procedure

When a defect or nonconformity is identified:

1. Record the issue in the designated log or relevant Asana task.
2. Assign a unique reference.
3. Assess severity and potential impact.
4. Assign a responsible owner.
5. Determine corrective action.
6. Escalate material or critical issues.
7. Complete the corrective work.
8. Perform retesting or reinspection.
9. Record the verification result.
10. Close the item only when the correction has been verified.

A defect must not be closed solely because a correction has been reported as completed.

### 7.5 Release and Acceptance Restrictions

The following conditions prevent normal deliverable acceptance or production release:

- Unresolved Critical defects.
- Material unresolved security findings.
- Unauthorized or materially inaccurate corporate service claims.
- Unresolved legal concerns that prevent authorized use of contractual templates.
- Failure of required core website or business inquiry functionality.
- Missing authorization for material commercial commitments.
- Missing evidence required by the applicable acceptance criteria.

Minor issues may be accepted as documented exceptions only when the authorized approver determines that they do not materially affect the intended purpose or introduce unacceptable risk.

All accepted exceptions must include an owner, disposition, and follow-up requirement where necessary.

## 10. Deliverable Acceptance and Sign-Off

### 10.1 Acceptance Process

The acceptance process consists of:

1. Submission of the completed deliverable by the Responsible role.
2. Review against the relevant Scope Baseline criteria.
3. Verification of supporting test and review evidence.
4. Identification and resolution or formal disposition of defects.
5. Preparation of the Deliverable Acceptance Record.
6. Approval by the designated authorized company representative.
7. Recording of the acceptance decision.

### 10.2 Acceptance Decision Types

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Accepted | The deliverable satisfies the required acceptance criteria and has been formally approved. |
| Accepted with Minor Exceptions | The deliverable is acceptable for its intended purpose, with minor documented issues approved for follow-up. |
| Rejected | The deliverable does not satisfy the required criteria and requires correction before acceptance. |
| Pending Review | Required verification or approval has not been completed. |

A deliverable must not be represented as accepted while its approval remains pending.

| D3 — Commercial & Legal Template Suite | External Legal Adviser and relevant business reviewers | Authorized Company Signatory or designated authorized representative |
| D4 — Operational Workspace | Project Manager and Technical Contributor where relevant | Project Manager, subject to any required Sponsor or company approval |
| D5 — Digital Service Catalogue | Marketing Contributor, Project Manager, and relevant business reviewer | Authorized Company Representative |
| D6 — Client Outreach Package | Marketing Contributor and Project Manager | Project Sponsor or authorized company representative |

## 13. Quality Monitoring, Change Control, and Continuous Improvement

### 13.1 Quality Monitoring Frequency

Quality will be monitored:

- During requirements and planning reviews.
- At relevant work-package completion points.
- Before website and service catalogue publication.
- Before commercial and legal templates are authorized for use.
- At the eight project milestone review points.
- During deliverable acceptance.
- During asset handover and project closure.
- Whenever a material defect, nonconformity, or quality risk is identified.

The Project Manager will review open quality actions during regular project coordination and weekly status reporting.

### 13.2 Quality-Related Risks

Quality-related risks must be recorded in the Risk Register (GEN-RSK-001).

Relevant risks may include:

- Technical misconfiguration.
- Cybersecurity weaknesses.
- Inaccurate corporate or service information.
- Inadequate legal and commercial review.
- Unclear acceptance criteria.
- Insufficient testing.
- Contractor dependency.
- Incomplete technical documentation.
- Inadequate asset handover.
- Unresolved privacy or regulatory requirements.

Material quality risks must be escalated according to the Communication Management Plan.

### 13.3 Quality-Related Change Control

A proposed change affecting quality requirements, acceptance criteria, testing scope, technical specifications, or deliverable standards must be documented and assessed.

The Project Manager will assess its impact on:

- Project scope.
- Schedule.
- Budget.
- Resources.
- Technical and operational requirements.
- Legal, security, and regulatory considerations.
- Related deliverables and acceptance criteria.

Material changes must receive approval from the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative.

Approved changes must be reflected in relevant project documents and quality records.

### 13.4 Continuous Improvement

The Project Manager will coordinate continuous improvement activities using:

- Lessons learned from completed work packages.
- Defect and nonconformity analysis.
- Stakeholder feedback.
- Technical and operational review findings.
- Acceptance outcomes.
- Repeated issues and root-cause analysis.
- Corrective-action effectiveness reviews.

The purpose is not only to correct individual defects but also to improve the company's future project delivery and operational practices.

### 13.5 Lessons Learned Integration

Quality lessons must be documented during the project rather than postponed entirely until closure.

The final Lessons Learned Retrospective will consider:

- Which quality checks were effective.
- Which defects or nonconformities occurred.
- Whether acceptance criteria were sufficiently clear.
- Whether review and approval activities occurred on time.
- Whether technical and professional resources were adequate.
- Whether evidence was recorded consistently.
- What changes should be incorporated into future company procedures.

## 14. Assumptions, Constraints, and Limitations

### 14.1 Assumptions

This plan is based on the following assumptions:

1. The seven deliverables remain consistent with Scope Baseline GEN-SBL-001.
2. Quality requirements will remain aligned with the latest approved project scope and technical architecture.
3. Appropriate contributors will be assigned or engaged for specialist verification activities.
4. Qualified legal advice will be available where required.
5. The Project Sponsor or authorized company representative will provide the necessary acceptance and approval decisions after formal designation.
6. Appropriate testing environments and evidence storage locations will be available.
7. Quality-related expenditure will be assessed within the authorized project budget.
8. The project will use a proportionate quality management approach appropriate to its size and operational requirements.
9. Public repository materials will be reviewed for confidentiality and security before publication.
10. The Quality Management Plan will be reviewed whenever material project requirements change.

### 14.2 Constraints

- The project's formal quality approval authority has not yet been fully established.
- Specialist technical and professional resources remain subject to confirmation.
- The planned eight-week schedule and resource availability have not been formally validated.
- The preliminary budget estimate remains subject to detailed cost estimation.
- External legal and regulatory reviews may affect deliverable readiness.
- Some verification activities require production or provider environments that may incur cost.
- The public GitHub repository cannot serve as the storage location for confidential quality evidence.

### 14.3 Limitations

This plan does not establish:

- A certified Quality Management System.
- Formal ISO 9001 certification.
- Formal security certification.
- Comprehensive penetration-testing assurance.
- Legal compliance certification.
- External audit or independent third-party assurance.
- Guaranteed operational performance beyond the defined project acceptance criteria.

Any additional quality assurance or certification requirement must be separately scoped, estimated, authorized, and documented.
