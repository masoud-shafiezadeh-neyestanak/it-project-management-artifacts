## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the preliminary budget framework for Project Genesis.

It identifies the main cost categories, defines the cost estimation and monitoring approach, records the current preliminary project cost estimate, and describes the approval and change-control requirements.

The budget will become an approved cost baseline only after detailed estimates have been reviewed and formally authorized by the Project Sponsor.

## 3. Budget Scope

The project budget covers costs directly associated with the approved Project Genesis scope and its seven principal deliverables:

- D1 — Digital Infrastructure
- D2 — Corporate Web Presence
- D3 — Commercial & Legal Template Suite
- D4 — Operational Workspace
- D5 — Digital Service Catalogue
- D6 — Client Outreach Package
- D7 — Project Closeout Package

Costs arising from out-of-scope activities, including unapproved integrations, payment gateway implementation, additional infrastructure, or major scope changes, are excluded unless authorized through the formal change-control process.

## 5. Cost Classification

Project costs will be classified to support accurate budgeting and future operational planning.

### 5.1 One-Time Costs

Expenses incurred once during project implementation, such as:

- Initial domain registration, where applicable.
- Website development or setup fees.
- External legal review.
- Initial design and content preparation.
- One-time configuration or implementation services.

### 5.2 Recurring Costs

Expenses that continue after implementation, such as:

- Corporate email and productivity subscriptions.
- Website hosting and domain renewal.
- Cloud infrastructure consumption.
- Project-management or collaboration subscriptions.
- Ongoing security, monitoring, and maintenance services.

Recurring costs must be evaluated separately from one-time implementation expenses to identify the financial commitments that may continue after project closure.

### 5.3 Internal and In-Kind Resources

Internal management, technical, and marketing effort should be recorded separately from external cash expenditure.

Where internal resources are provided without an additional cash payment, the relevant effort should not automatically be treated as a zero-cost economic resource.

Any decision to include internal labor in the formal project budget must be documented consistently.

## 6. Cost Estimation Method

The project will use a bottom-up estimation approach where sufficient information is available.

The estimation process will include:

1. Identifying the resources and services required by each applicable WBS work package.
2. Obtaining current supplier quotations or published subscription prices.
3. Estimating usage-based cloud and hosting costs using the expected configuration and consumption assumptions.
4. Separating one-time costs from recurring commitments.
5. Recording taxes, transaction fees, and currency assumptions where applicable.
6. Reviewing estimates for omissions, overlaps, and double-counting.
7. Consolidating the estimates for Sponsor review and approval.

Where reliable pricing information is unavailable, the estimate will be marked as provisional rather than presented as a confirmed amount.

## 7. Contingency and Management Reserves

### 7.1 Contingency Reserve

A contingency reserve may be established to address identified risks that could result in additional project costs.

The reserve should be determined after reviewing the Risk Register (GEN-RSK-001) and the cost estimates associated with the relevant risk responses.

No numerical contingency reserve has been approved at this stage.

### 7.2 Management Reserve

Management reserve, if required, will be considered separately from the cost baseline and will remain subject to Sponsor authorization.

It must not be treated as an automatically available project expenditure allowance.

## 9. Budget Monitoring and Control

The Lead Project Manager will maintain a cost tracking record throughout project execution.

The record should include:

- Approved budget, once authorized.
- Actual expenditure.
- Outstanding purchase commitments.
- Forecast cost to complete.
- Estimated final project cost.
- Variance between approved budget and forecast expenditure.
- Supporting invoices, quotations, or payment records where applicable.

Budget performance will be reviewed alongside schedule, scope, risk, and deliverable status.

### 9.1 Cost Variance

Once an approved cost baseline exists, cost variance will be monitored using:

**Cost Variance (CV) = Approved Budget for Work Performed (EV) − Actual Cost (AC)**

A negative cost variance indicates that the work performed has cost more than its corresponding approved earned value.

Where sufficient progress and cost data are available, additional earned value indicators may be introduced. They will not be reported as meaningful performance measures until the required data is being consistently collected.

### 9.2 Escalation

Any forecast expenditure exceeding the approved cost baseline, or any anticipated commitment that would materially affect the approved budget, must be reported to the Sponsor.

Potential overspending must not be resolved by silently reducing scope, bypassing approvals, or committing unapproved expenditure.

## 10. Budget Change Control

Any proposed change affecting the approved budget must be documented and reviewed before implementation.

A budget change request should include:

- Description and reason for the proposed change.
- Relevant WBS work package.
- Estimated cost impact.
- Schedule and scope implications.
- Associated risks and alternatives.
- Recommended action.
- Required approval.

Changes to the approved cost baseline require Sponsor authorization.

Approved changes must be reflected in the budget records and associated project documentation.

## 11. Budget Assumptions and Constraints

The preliminary budget framework is based on the following assumptions:

1. The project is planned for an eight-calendar-week implementation period, subject to confirmation of the project start date and working calendar.
2. The seven deliverables remain within the approved project scope.
3. Existing organizational accounts, tools, and subscriptions will be reused where suitable and authorized.
4. Cloud and website services will be selected with consideration for cost, security, operational suitability, and future maintenance.
5. No payment gateway implementation is included in the current scope.
6. External legal review costs must be confirmed through an appropriate quotation or written fee estimate.
7. Recurring subscription and infrastructure costs will be identified separately from one-time setup costs.
8. Applicable taxes, foreign currency charges, and payment processing fees must be verified before final approval.
9. Any significant scope, technical, or supplier change may require a revised cost estimate.
