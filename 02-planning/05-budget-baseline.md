## 1. Purpose and Budget Management Objectives

### 1.1 Purpose

This document establishes the preliminary financial planning and cost management framework for Project Genesis, a real business and operational readiness initiative undertaken within Bridge of Tomorrow, an Oman-registered company with an active Commercial Registration (CR).

It identifies anticipated project cost categories, establishes a consistent estimation approach, distinguishes one-time and recurring expenditure, defines budget monitoring requirements, and documents approval and change-control arrangements.

The document is intended to become the authorized Cost Baseline after detailed estimates, resource requirements, funding arrangements, and applicable approval authorities have been reviewed and formally approved.

### 1.2 Budget Management Objectives

The objectives are to:

- Establish a transparent financial estimate for the seven project deliverables.
- Identify direct project expenditure and relevant recurring operating commitments.
- Distinguish confirmed costs, estimated costs, unquoted items, and in-kind contributions.
- Obtain supplier quotations and verifiable pricing information.
- Estimate cloud and software costs using actual configurations and expected consumption.
- Prevent duplicate allocation of shared costs.
- Maintain traceable records of expenditure and purchase commitments.
- Monitor actual costs against the authorized budget.
- Forecast the estimated final project cost.
- Identify and escalate potential cost overruns.
- Establish a risk-based contingency reserve where justified.
- Maintain clear financial decision rights and expenditure authorization controls.

### 1.3 Budget Baseline Components

The proposed Budget Baseline consists of:

1. Detailed cost estimates for applicable WBS work packages.
2. Cost assumptions, pricing sources, quantities, and estimation methods.
3. One-time and recurring cost classifications.
4. Direct project cash expenditure.
5. Applicable taxes, transaction charges, and foreign currency costs.
6. Contractor and professional advisory fees.
7. A risk-based contingency reserve, where approved.
8. Authorized expenditure limits and approval rules.
9. Cost monitoring, forecasting, and change-control arrangements.

The Cost Baseline is maintained separately from the Scope Baseline (GEN-SBL-001) and Schedule Baseline (GEN-SCH-001), while remaining consistent with both.

### 5.3 Usage-Based Costs

Existing management effort and internal resources must be recorded separately from external cash expenditure.

## 6. Cost Estimation and Validation Process

### 6.1 Bottom-Up Estimation

The project will use bottom-up estimation where sufficient information is available.

The process includes:

1. Reviewing each WBS work package.
2. Identifying required products, services, personnel, and subscriptions.
3. Determining quantities and expected usage.
4. Obtaining supplier quotations, official prices, or resource estimates.
5. Calculating one-time, recurring, and usage-based costs.
6. Identifying taxes and transaction charges.
7. Reviewing potential cost overlaps.
8. Identifying costs associated with material project risks.
9. Consolidating estimates.
10. Submitting the proposed budget for Sponsor review and approval.

Where reliable pricing is unavailable, the estimate must remain provisional.

### 6.2 Estimation Evidence

| Evidence Type | Intended Use |
|---|---|
| Supplier Quotation | Confirmed pricing for a defined product or service |
| Official Provider Pricing | Reference pricing for subscriptions and cloud services |
| AWS Pricing Calculator | Workload-specific cloud cost estimation |
| Historical Invoice | Verification of current recurring or actual charges |
| Contractor Proposal | Estimation of technical or professional service costs |
| Internal Resource Estimate | Documented effort and resource assumptions |
| Tax or Professional Advice | Confirmation of applicable financial treatment |
| Previous Purchase Record | Historical reference, subject to current verification |

A published price is not automatically a supplier quotation or purchasing commitment.

### 6.3 AWS Cost Estimation

AWS services must be estimated using the intended architecture and expected usage.

The estimate should identify:

- AWS Region.
- Relevant services.
- Compute configuration and runtime.
- Storage quantities.
- Expected data transfer.
- API requests and serverless execution volume.
- Backup and monitoring requirements.
- Data retention requirements.
- Applicable taxes and charges not included in provider estimates.

The AWS Pricing Calculator may be used:

[AWS Pricing Calculator](https://calculator.aws/)

[AWS Pricing Calculator Documentation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/pricing-calculator/latest/userguide/getting-started.html)

Calculator outputs are estimates rather than guaranteed final charges. AWS Free Tier benefits or promotional credits must not be assumed unless eligibility is verified for the relevant account and services.

All new cloud resources must be reviewed for potential charges before deployment.

### 6.4 Google Workspace and SaaS Cost Estimation

The estimate must verify:

- Existing subscription status.
- Number of required licenses.
- Selected plan and included features.
- Monthly or annual commitment.
- Additional user or storage charges.
- Applicable taxes.
- Renewal and cancellation terms.
- Whether the cost is already included in existing company expenditure.

Pricing references:

- [Google Workspace Pricing](https://workspace.google.com/pricing)
- [Omantel — Google Workspace](https://portal.omantel.om/en/business/google-workspace)

The same verification approach applies to Asana, GitHub, domain registration, hosting, and other SaaS services.

### 6.5 Legal and Professional Service Estimation

External legal review costs must be based on a written fee quotation or rate card, agreed review scope, document volume, review rounds, any required localization, and applicable taxes.

No legal fee amount is assumed or presented as confirmed.

## 9. Budget Monitoring and Cost Control

### 9.1 Cost Tracking

The Project Manager will maintain a cost tracking record containing:

| Cost Element | Description |
|---|---|
| Estimated Cost | Current estimate for the item |
| Approved Budget | Formally authorized allocation, once established |
| Committed Cost | Amount contractually committed or otherwise obligated |
| Actual Cost | Amount invoiced or paid, with reporting basis identified |
| Remaining Commitment | Outstanding amount under existing commitment |
| Forecast Cost to Complete | Expected additional cost to complete the scope |
| Estimated Final Cost | Forecast total project cost |
| Cost Variance | Difference between applicable budget or earned value measure and actual or forecast expenditure |
| Supporting Evidence | Quotation, contract, invoice, approval, or payment record |

Actual and committed expenditure must not be combined in a way that causes double-counting.

### 9.2 Cost Review Frequency

Cost information will be reviewed:

- At least weekly during active project execution.
- Before material purchases or contractor commitments.
- When supplier prices change.
- When a risk creates additional cost exposure.
- When a scope or schedule change is proposed.
- At major milestones.
- During final budget reconciliation.

### 9.3 Cost Performance Measures

Once a Cost Baseline and reliable progress measurement are available:

**Cost Variance (CV)**

CV = Earned Value (EV) − Actual Cost (AC)

A negative CV indicates that the cost of work performed exceeds its corresponding budgeted value.

**Cost Performance Index (CPI)**

CPI = EV ÷ AC

A CPI below 1.0 indicates that less budgeted value has been earned for each unit of actual cost.

**Estimated Cost at Completion (EAC)**

Where current cost efficiency is expected to continue and the assumptions are valid:

EAC = BAC ÷ CPI

Earned Value measures must not be reported as meaningful performance indicators until an approved budget, measurable work-completion rules, and consistent actual cost data exist.

Before that point, the project will use estimates, commitments, actual expenditure, forecasts, and documented variances.

### 9.4 Budget Variance Escalation

| Condition | Required Action |
|---|---|
| Minor difference with no material budget impact | Project Manager records and monitors the variance |
| Unquoted cost or forecast increase affecting a work package | Obtain updated pricing and assess impact |
| Forecast final cost exceeds approved Cost Baseline | Escalate to authorized company representative |
| Purchase exceeds delegated spending authority | Obtain approval before commitment |
| High financial risk or material unplanned exposure | Escalate and assess corrective action |
| Scope change with financial implications | Submit a Change Request |

No numerical cost tolerance is established in this draft.

### 9.5 Cloud Cost Controls

For AWS and other usage-based cloud services:

- Configure appropriate billing access and contacts.
- Activate billing alerts.
- Use AWS Budgets where appropriate.
- Monitor usage through Billing and Cost Explorer.
- Review resources that may incur ongoing charges.
- Avoid leaving unnecessary test or development resources running.
- Apply cost allocation tags where practical.
- Review storage, logging, transfer, and backup consumption.
- Document expected monthly operating costs.
- Verify actual billing arrangements and eligibility for credits or discounts.

Cost alerts do not automatically prevent expenditure.

The Project Manager and assigned Technical Contributor must review abnormal consumption and arrange corrective action within the appropriate authority.
