## 1. Purpose and Budget Management Objectives

### 1.1 Purpose

This document establishes the preliminary financial planning and cost management framework for Project Genesis, a real business and operational readiness initiative undertaken within Bridge of Tomorrow, an Oman-registered company with an active Commercial Registration (CR).

The purpose is to identify anticipated project cost categories, establish a consistent estimation approach, distinguish one-time and recurring expenditure, define budget monitoring requirements, and document approval and change-control arrangements.

The document is intended to become the authorized Cost Baseline after detailed estimates, resource requirements, funding arrangements, and applicable approval authorities have been reviewed and formally approved.

### 1.2 Budget Management Objectives

The budget management objectives are to:

- Establish a transparent financial estimate for the seven project deliverables.
- Identify direct project expenditure and relevant recurring operating commitments.
- Distinguish confirmed costs, estimated costs, unquoted items, and in-kind contributions.
- Obtain supplier quotations and verifiable pricing information wherever practicable.
- Estimate cloud and software costs using actual configurations and expected consumption.
- Prevent duplicate allocation of shared costs across work packages.
- Maintain traceable records of approved expenditure and purchase commitments.
- Monitor actual costs against the applicable authorized budget.
- Forecast the estimated final project cost.
- Identify and escalate potential cost overruns.
- Establish a risk-based contingency reserve where justified.
- Maintain clear financial decision rights and expenditure authorization controls.

### 1.3 Budget Baseline Components

The proposed Budget Baseline will consist of:

1. Detailed cost estimates for applicable WBS work packages.
2. Cost assumptions, pricing sources, quantities, and estimation methods.
3. One-time and recurring cost classifications.
4. Direct project cash expenditure.
5. Relevant taxes, transaction charges, and foreign currency costs.
6. Contractor and professional advisory fees.
7. A risk-based contingency reserve, where approved.
8. Authorized project expenditure limits and financial approval rules.
9. Cost monitoring, forecasting, and change-control arrangements.

The Cost Baseline is maintained separately from the Scope Baseline (GEN-SBL-001) and Schedule Baseline (GEN-SCH-001), while remaining consistent with both.

## 5. Cost Classification and Estimation Method

### 5.1 One-Time Costs

One-time costs are expenditures expected to occur once during the project implementation period.

Examples may include:

- Initial domain registration.
- Website development and initial implementation.
- External legal review of standard templates.
- Initial design and content preparation.
- One-time technical configuration or setup.
- Approved initial security or testing services.

One-time costs must be supported by a quotation, invoice, or documented estimate.

### 5.2 Recurring Costs

Recurring costs are expenses that continue beyond the initial implementation.

Examples include:

- Corporate email and productivity subscriptions.
- Domain renewal.
- Website hosting.
- Cloud infrastructure consumption.
- Project-management tool subscriptions.
- Backup and monitoring services.
- Software and security subscriptions.

The project budget must account for the cost expected during the implementation period.

Post-project recurring financial commitments must also be disclosed separately to support operational planning and future budgeting.

Where a provider requires an annual subscription or minimum commitment, the full contractual commitment must be disclosed even when only part of the service period falls within the project schedule.

### 5.3 Usage-Based Costs

Usage-based costs are expenses that depend on actual consumption.

Examples include:

- AWS compute and serverless execution.
- Object storage and database consumption.
- Data transfer and network requests.
- Monitoring and logging services.
- API requests or external integrations.
- Additional cloud storage or backup consumption.

These costs must be estimated using the intended service configuration, expected workload, region, storage, requests, and consumption assumptions.

Uncontrolled or unexpected consumption must be monitored through appropriate alerts and cost-management controls.

### 5.4 Contractor and Professional Service Costs

External resources may be engaged for:

- Technical implementation.
- Website design and development.
- Arabic and English content preparation or review.
- Legal review of commercial and contractual templates.
- Specialist security or privacy advice.
- Other approved implementation activities.

Estimates must be based on an agreed scope of work, quotation, rate card, or documented estimate.

Any contractor commitment must define the required outputs, ownership arrangements, payment terms, and handover obligations.

### 5.5 Internal and In-Kind Resources

Existing management effort and internal company resources should be recorded separately from external cash expenditure.

Where internal personnel perform project work without an additional cash payment, their effort may still represent an economic resource cost.

For transparency:

- Cash expenditure must be reported separately.
- Internal effort may be reported as an in-kind or allocated resource contribution.
- Any labor cost included in the formal cost estimate must use a consistent and documented rate or valuation method.
- The same internal effort must not be counted both as an external cash cost and as an in-kind contribution.

## 6. Cost Estimation and Validation Process

### 6.1 Bottom-Up Estimation

The project will use a bottom-up cost estimation approach where sufficient information is available.

The process includes:

1. Reviewing each WBS work package.
2. Identifying required products, services, personnel, subscriptions, and other resources.
3. Determining quantities and expected usage.
4. Obtaining supplier quotations, official pricing, or documented resource estimates.
5. Calculating one-time, recurring, and usage-based costs.
6. Identifying applicable taxes and financial transaction charges.
7. Reviewing potential cost overlaps.
8. Identifying costs associated with material project risks.
9. Consolidating the estimates into the Budget Baseline.
10. Submitting the proposed budget for Sponsor review and formal approval.

Where sufficient pricing data is unavailable, the estimate must remain marked as provisional.

## 9. Budget Monitoring and Cost Control

### 9.1 Cost Tracking

The Project Manager will maintain a cost tracking record throughout project execution.

The record should contain:

| Cost Element | Description |
|---|---|
| Estimated Cost | Current estimate for the applicable cost item |
| Approved Budget | Formally authorized budget allocation, once established |
| Committed Cost | Amount contractually committed or otherwise formally obligated |
| Actual Cost | Amount invoiced or paid, with the financial reporting basis clearly identified |
| Remaining Commitment | Outstanding amount under an existing commitment |
| Forecast Cost to Complete | Expected additional cost required to complete the approved scope |

| Cost Variance | Difference between the applicable approved budget or earned value measure and actual or forecast expenditure |
| Supporting Evidence | Relevant quotation, contract, invoice, approval, or payment record |

Actual expenditure and committed expenditure must not be combined in a way that causes double-counting.

### 9.2 Cost Review Frequency

The Project Manager will review project cost information:

- At least weekly during active project execution.
- Before material purchase or contractor commitments.
- When a quotation or supplier price changes.
- When a project risk creates additional cost exposure.
- When a material scope or schedule change is proposed.
- At each major project milestone.
- During project closure and final budget reconciliation.

### 9.3 Cost Performance Measures

Once a Cost Baseline and appropriate progress measurement arrangements are approved, cost performance may be monitored using the following measures.

**Cost Variance (CV)**

CV = Earned Value (EV) − Actual Cost (AC)

Earned Value represents the budgeted value of work actually completed.

A negative CV indicates that the cost of work performed exceeds the corresponding budgeted value of that work.

**Cost Performance Index (CPI)**

CPI = EV ÷ AC

A CPI below 1.0 indicates that less budgeted value has been earned for each unit of actual cost.

**Estimated Cost at Completion (EAC)**

Where the relevant Earned Value Management assumptions are valid and reliable performance data exists, EAC may be calculated using an appropriate forecasting method.

For example, where current cost efficiency is expected to continue:

EAC = BAC ÷ CPI

Earned Value measures must not be reported as meaningful performance indicators until the approved budget, measurable work completion rules, and actual cost data are consistently available.

Before that point, the project will rely on estimate-versus-actual comparisons, commitments, forecast costs, and documented variances.

### 9.4 Budget Variance Escalation

The following escalation principles apply:

| Condition | Required Action |
|---|---|
| Minor difference between estimate and actual cost with no material budget impact | Project Manager records and monitors the variance. |
| Unquoted cost or forecast increase affecting a work package | Project Manager obtains updated pricing and assesses the impact. |
| Forecast final cost exceeds the approved Cost Baseline | Escalate to the Project Sponsor or authorized company representative. |
| Proposed purchase exceeds delegated spending authority | Obtain formal authorization before commitment. |
| High financial risk or material unplanned cost exposure | Escalate and assess corrective action or change control. |
| Material scope change with financial implications | Submit a Change Request with cost and schedule impact assessment. |

No numerical cost variance tolerance is established in this draft. Any formal tolerance must be approved together with the Budget Baseline.

### 9.5 Cloud Cost Controls

- Activate relevant billing alerts.
- Use AWS Budgets to define appropriate cost thresholds.
